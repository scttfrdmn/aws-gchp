GATE 5f-N — is the window x handles PROXY tight? (lith#301, instrument #303)
in-region, head node already up, ~$0.01 of GETs
PRE-REGISTERED 2026-10-01 BEFORE THE DECISIVE CELLS RAN

WHY THIS GATE AND WHY ME
  #303 landed the gauge that makes #301 answerable: lith_prefetch_resident_bytes, the
  quantity --prefetch-budget actually bounds, as against the window x handles PROXY that
  enforces it. Upstream stated the decision rule and then said it needs a box they had
  been deferring, and explicitly asked me for nothing ("Nothing of yours... I'd rather
  bank what you've found than ask for more"). I have the box up and the 5f-M harness, so
  this is their experiment for ~$0.01 instead of a cluster.

  Their rule, quoted: "If resident sits far below 4.128 GB at N=1, the proxy is
  over-conservative and the divisor is costing 6.38x for nothing. If it sits near it,
  candidate 1 is dangerous and candidate 2 is the answer."

ARM   5f-M arm B shape, the netCDF/HEMCO shape: 1 streaming reader (dd, 3.78 GB object)
      + (N-1) descriptors held open after a 4 KiB header read and never streamed.
      N in 1, 2, 4, 16, 64, 256. Upstream's 1/16/256 plus trend points, free.
      3 reps, fresh cold mount per cell, default flags (8 MiB blocks, auto window 223).

ALL FOUR QUANTITIES ARE MEASURED FROM THE MOUNT'S OWN GAUGES, NONE DERIVED BY ME
      lith_prefetch_resident_bytes    held       (#303)
      lith_prefetch_budget_bytes      the limit  (#303)
      lith_readahead_window_blocks    realized window (#302)
      lith_open_handles               the divisor     (#302)
  scraped at 10 Hz for the life of each cell, sampler started BEFORE the reader so the
  establishment transient is in the series.
  tightness = resident / (window x handles x block_size).
  This matters because 5f-M's withdrawn result was a DERIVATION from a cumulative
  counter and it returned the charge. Nothing here is cumulative and nothing is derived.

SMOKE CELL ALREADY RUN (N=1, 1 rep) — reported here because it is what the predictions
are calibrated against, and it must not be presented later as a confirmation:
      budget gauge reads 4.127829504e9 EXACTLY  (= 33.022636032 GB x 25% x 50%,
        confirming the arithmetic I asserted on #298 to the byte)
      resident steps 0 -> 1870.7 MB in ONE 100 ms sample, then holds flat
        223 blocks x 8 MiB = 1870.659584 MB -> the establishment burst of 5f-M seen by
        a correct instrument, and the first independent confirmation of it
      resident drains to 0 at unmount -> the add/remove paths balance in a REAL run.
        #303's tests only assert this on constructed state.

PREDICTIONS, AND WHAT EACH OUTCOME WOULD MEAN

  H_headroom  resident peaks at ~1871 MB at N=1 = 45.3% of the 4127.8 MB budget, and
              LOWER at every N > 1. Upstream's first branch fires: the proxy is
              over-conservative, and even its tightest case leaves the budget 55% empty.
              -> If instead resident approaches 4128 MB at any N, the proxy is tight,
                 candidate 1 is dangerous, and H_oneover dies with it.

  H_oneover   ONLY the streaming handle dispatches, so resident tracks ONE window while
              the charge is window x N:
                  resident(N) ~ window(N) x 8 MiB      and     tightness ~ 1/N
              Point predictions, from window = clamp(492/N, 2, 223) measured in 5f-M:
                  N=1    win 223  resident ~1871 MB  charge 1871 MB  tight ~1.00
                  N=2    win 223  resident ~1871 MB  charge 3741 MB  tight ~0.50
                  N=4    win 123  resident ~1032 MB  charge 4128 MB  tight ~0.25
                  N=16   win  30  resident ~ 252 MB  charge 4027 MB  tight ~0.063
                  N=64   win   7  resident ~  59 MB  charge 3758 MB  tight ~0.016
                  N=256  win   2  resident ~  17 MB  charge 4295 MB  tight ~0.004
              -> This is the quantitative form of 5f-M's "idle handles dispatch exactly
                 zero". If it holds, the divisor throttles a mount to 186 MB/s while it
                 holds 0.4% of the bytes it is allowed to hold, and the proxy's error is
                 not a constant factor but LINEAR IN THE IDLE DESCRIPTOR COUNT.

  REFUTER     resident within 2x of the budget at N=16 or N=256. That kills H_oneover and
              H_headroom together, says the proxy is doing real work at high N, and makes
              candidate 2 the answer. I will report it as the result if it fires.

  SECOND REFUTER  resident EXCEEDS 4128 MB at any N. Then the budget is not enforced by
              this path at all and something else bounds prefetch; the whole #301 framing
              would need re-deriving.

CAVEAT, STATED UP FRONT BECAUSE IT CUTS TOWARD THE FINDING RATHER THAN AWAY
  blockstore.go:809 marks a chunk prefetched at DISPATCH (LoadOrStore before the fill);
  dropPrefetched removes it on consume / evict-unread / failed-fill. So the gauge counts
  dispatched-and-unconsumed, which INCLUDES bytes still in flight. It is an UPPER BOUND
  on bytes resident in RAM. A finding that it sits far below the budget is therefore
  conservative; a finding that it sits near the budget would NOT be, and I would have to
  separate in-flight from arrived before claiming it.

LIMITS KNOWN BEFORE RUNNING
  In-region (2.2 ms), one box, one object family, contiguous dd, 8 MiB blocks, n=3.
  Resident is sampled at 10 Hz, so a transient shorter than 100 ms is invisible; the
  establishment burst is a single step and survives that, a drain spike might not.
  This measures whether the proxy is tight, NOT whether candidate 1 is safe -- the
  re-grant churn objection upstream raised from my own 5f-M result is untouched by it.

RESULT 1 — H_headroom CONFIRMED. The budget is never close to full, not even once.
      N=1 is the proxy's TIGHTEST case (tightness exactly 1.000) and it holds
      1870.7 MB of a 4127.8 MB budget = 45.3%. Every larger N holds less.
      Upstream's FIRST branch fires: the proxy is over-conservative.

RESULT 2 — H_oneover CONFIRMED to within 0.0-6.2% at every point

  Resident tracks ONE window regardless of how many descriptors are charged for it, so
      tightness = 1/N
  and the proxy's error is not a constant factor but LINEAR IN THE OPEN DESCRIPTOR COUNT.
  At N=256 the mount is charged 4295.0 MB and holds 17.8 MB: a 241x overstatement.
  This is the quantitative form of 5f-M's "idle handles dispatch exactly zero" -- same
  conclusion, reached with an instrument that measures instead of deriving.

RESULT 3 — WHAT THE OVER-CONSERVATISM COSTS
      N=1   walls 2.98 / 3.24 / 3.16 s   median  3.158 s
      N=256 walls 36.21 / 30.15 / 28.21 s median 30.151 s   =  9.5x
  So the mount is throttled ~9.5x while holding 0.43% of the bytes it is permitted to
  hold, on a budget that is 99.6% empty.

  CORRECTION TO MY OWN PUBLISHED NUMBER, with the control that forced it: 5f-M reported
  6.38x for this comparison (3.187 -> 20.343 s). The two gates agree at N=1 (3.16 vs
  3.19) and disagree at N=256 (20.3 vs 30.2). I A/B'd the binary to find out -- the
  pre-#299 build lith-new, re-run through THIS harness, gives 31.20 / 30.99 / 30.16 s,
  median 30.99 s, statistically identical to lith-303's 30.151 s. So the binary is
  cleared (no regression from #299/#302/#303) and the 20.3 s was between-gate variance.
  The window-2 cell is LATENCY-BOUND -- 465 GETs with 2 in flight -- so it is sensitive
  to per-GET latency drift, which makes it a poor cell to quote a precise multiple from.
  The honest statement is 6-10x, and "6.38x", which upstream has now quoted twice,
  should be read as the low end of a range rather than a measurement.

RESULT 4 — THE THRASH THE DIVISOR EXISTS TO PREVENT DID NOT OCCUR AT ANY N
      lith_prefetch_evicted_unread_total = 0 in 18/18 cells
      issued == used == 3586 in 18/18 -> 100% of prefetched chunks were consumed
  Including at N=1, where residency is highest at 1870.7 MB.
  LIMIT, and it is the important one: this is ONE streaming reader. #55's thrash shape is
  not represented here at all. This does NOT show the divisor is unnecessary in general.
  It shows that on the shape that motivated #256 -- a netCDF reader holding many
  descriptors -- the divisor's protection bought nothing and cost ~9.5x.

RESULT 5 — A THIRD OPTION THAT DID NOT EXIST BEFORE #303
  #301's candidates both ration the proxy: candidate 1 stops charging for idle handles
  (which upstream argued against using my own 5f-M re-grant finding -- the divisor would
  then change on every establish/de-establish, multiplying ded_W+1 bursts), candidate 2
  charges per object rather than per descriptor (which only fixes double-open).
  The gauge makes a third possible: ration on the MEASURED resident bytes.

  What this gate contributes to weighing it, measured rather than argued:
    - resident does not move when an idle descriptor opens or closes. At N=256, with 255
      descriptors opening before the read, resident never left 15.7-24.1 MB. The churn
      source that defeats candidate 1 is absent from this signal by construction.
    - the signal is steady enough to govern on: middle-50% spread of the resident series
      is 0.71% (N=4), 2.89% (N=16), 9.58% (N=1), 12.62% (N=2), 13.79% (N=64). At N=256
      it is 47%, but there the magnitude is 2 blocks, so that is one block of jitter.
  NOT claimed: that this is safe. Admission on resident bytes still has to choose a
  window for a newly establishing handle, and 5f-M showed that commitment is a
  single-shot full window -- so the open transient is unchanged. And per the caveat
  above, the gauge counts bytes at dispatch, so using it for admission rations
  dispatched-including-in-flight, which is arguably the right quantity for admission but
  is NOT bytes-in-RAM. Both belong in the decision; neither is settled by this gate.
