WHAT var1 SETTLES: COVERAGE, NOT SEQUENTIALITY
  var1 is monotone start-to-finish within its variable — the most sequential
  access in the whole set — and lands at FT 0.005 (met) / 0.083 (hco), the
  second- and third-worst of six. Reading one variable of five fetches the WHOLE
  object: over-fetch 54.03x against coverage 0.01843, whose reciprocal is 54.26 —
  the over-fetch IS 1/coverage, to 0.4%. Being sequential does not rescue a reader that only wants a small
  fraction of the object; it is what dooms it, because sequential is precisely
  what earns it the full window.

TWO WASTE CHANNELS, BOTH SELECTED BY LOW COVERAGE, ONLY ONE FIXED BY #256's FLAG
  A  window over-commitment: looks sequential, wants little => whole-object fetch.
     var1 arms. evidence_ratio=4 cuts met/var1 by 95.1% of bytes (1217.8 -> 59.8 MB,
     over-fetch 54.03 -> 2.65) and hco/var1 by 56.3% (9.89 -> 4.32), while RAISING
     follow-through (0.005 -> 0.135, 0.083 -> 0.190) and cutting GETs (175 -> 37,
     131 -> 87).
  B  cold-start granularity tax: small scattered reads, the #256 floor.
     sub arms. evidence_ratio=4 is BIT-IDENTICAL on every axis — same bytes, same
     GETs, same FT, same tax. The flag is inert against the floor, as it should be:
     the floor is a chunk-granularity commitment taken before any window decision.
  => coverage predicts THAT you over-fetch; read shape decides WHICH mechanism
     bills you, and the two need different fixes.

PRICE OF --readahead-evidence-ratio 4 ON THE SEQUENTIAL ARMS (the regression check)
  met/whole  bytes 1217.8 -> 1217.8 (+0.00%)  GETs 175 -> 176 (+0.6%)  FT 1.0000 -> 1.0000
  hco/whole  bytes  851.4 ->  852.0 (+0.07%)  GETs 130 -> 159 (+22.3%) FT 1.0000 -> 0.9948
  The cost is REQUESTS, not bytes: the window has to ramp, so a long sequential
  read pays up to ~22% more GETs for the same bytes. Wall clock moved -4.4% and
  +2.6%, i.e. inside noise at n=1 per arm. That is the trade upstream should be
  pricing on #256, and it looks cheap next to 95% of the bytes on the low-coverage side.

DISCLOSED REGIME DIFFERENCE (the honest caveat, and it cuts toward the finding)
  3 open handles here vs ~600 in capture 2. The window is
  clamp(prefetch_budget/open_handles, 2, max_readahead), so GCHP ran at the FLOOR
  of 2 and this workload runs at the full 223. That is why the low-coverage penalty
  is 54x here and 2.46x in GCHP: the 48-rank clamp was HIDING most of it. The sign
  replicates and the magnitude is worse in the single-handle regime that ordinary
  tools (python, ncks, xarray) actually run in. n=1 per arm on wall clock; bytes and
  GETs are counters, not timings, so they need no repetition.

INDEPENDENT REPLICATION OF THE SHARED-CACHE UNIT
  The shared (key, block) unit reproduced the mount's own lith_prefetch_issued_total
  at 1.00x on ALL SIX arms; the per-handle unit overstated by 10.58x (met/whole) and
  5.15x (hco/whole). Tighter than the 1.04-1.08x on GCHP.
  But for a DIFFERENT reason: cross-handle redemption is 0.0% here (84-85% in GCHP),
  so the per-handle overstatement is the SAME handle re-entering, not another handle
  sharing. The unit is right in both workloads; the mechanism that makes it right is
  not the same one. A per-handle governor has nothing to govern on in either case.
