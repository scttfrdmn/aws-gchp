GATE 5f-H — THE HIGH-RTT RAMP ARM (lith#256), 2026-09-30, ~$0.60 of egress
================================================================================

WHY: upstream proposed we stop trying to time --readahead-evidence-ratio, with one
exception — "unless you think a slow or high-latency endpoint (cross-region, a
non-AWS S3) would change the ramp story, in which case that's the one arm worth
adding, and it's cheap." Gate 5f-G found a wall cost that no counter carries
(+0.24 to +0.49 s, fixed in seconds, bytes and requests identical), and inferred
it was ramp LATENCY rather than ramp work. That inference makes a falsifiable
prediction about RTT, which was pre-registered on #256 before this ran:

  "If the cost is ramp latency rather than ramp work, it scales with RTT: at
   ~60 ms instead of ~1 ms I predict the fixed penalty grows by roughly the RTT
   ratio times the number of ramp steps — order SECONDS, not +0.35 s — while
   bytes and requests stay identical. If instead the penalty stays ~0.35 s, my
   mechanism is wrong and it's something local."

SETUP  the SAME object, server-side copied to us-west-2, read from the us-east-1
  head node. Only the endpoint distance changes.
    in-region    s3://gcgrid/HEMCO/GT_Chlorine/v2024-05/GT_Chlorine_...nc
    cross-region s3://gchp-lith-xregion-usw2-942542972736/hco/GT_Chlorine_...nc
  TCP connect RTT from the head node: 2.2 ms (us-east-1) vs 58.6 ms (us-west-2)
  = 26.7x. Readers dd (bs=1M) and pread (python os.pread, 1 MiB, increasing
  offsets); ratio {0,4}; n=8 per cell pooled over two launches (3 + 5 reps);
  fresh mount, cold cache and a unique metrics port per cell.

RESULT
  bytes and requests: IDENTICAL at both ratios, both readers, all 32 cells —
  851,394,200 B and 116 GETs everywhere. The byte/request settlement upstream
  proposed holds at 26.7x the RTT. That part is now tested, not assumed.

  wall (s), sorted, n=8 per cell:

          r4  4.25 4.40 4.96 11.13 11.65 15.31 19.74 24.18  median 11.39  (+7.09 s, +165%)
    pread r0  3.34 3.55 3.73 4.06 4.17 4.18 4.18 8.89      median 4.11
          r4  4.05 4.19 4.35 4.38 4.81 11.15 19.81 22.97    median  4.59  (+0.48 s, +12%)

1. THE PREDICTION PASSES ON MAGNITUDE AND FAILS ON SHAPE — both worth saying
   Order-seconds: YES on dd (+7.09 s median, 22x the in-region +0.32 s). But it
   is NOT a fixed penalty. The ratio-4 distribution is BIMODAL: a mode at the
   ratio-0 wall (~4.3 s) and a second mode at 11-24 s, with nothing between
   5.0 and 11.1 s on either reader. pread's median lands in the low mode, which
   is why its median delta is only +0.48 s while three of its eight reps are in
   the high mode. Reporting either reader's median alone misrepresents it.

   The distribution-free statement is the honest one:
     wall > 9 s   ratio 0: 0 of 16 cells      ratio 4: 8 of 16 cells
   (9 s is above every ratio-0 observation.) Fisher exact p ~ 7e-4. Rep order
   does not predict which mode a cell lands in, so it is not a warmup artifact.

2. SO THE MECHANISM IS A STALL MODE, NOT A TAX
   A fixed per-ramp latency cost would have scaled smoothly. A bimodal one says
   the ramp sometimes fails to escape: with the window at its floor and a 58 ms
   RTT, in-flight bytes fall below the bandwidth-delay product, so throughput
   becomes RTT-bound, so evidence accrues more slowly, so the window grows more
   slowly. Whether a given read escapes that loop is what is bimodal. The high
   mode runs at 35-75 MB/s against a ~200 MB/s baseline for identical bytes and
   identical request counts.
   I am asserting the DISTRIBUTION, not the loop: the feedback story is the
   simplest account of a bimodal wall with invariant counters, and it predicts
   that raising the floor (or seeding evidence from the first block's own size)
   should collapse the high mode. That is upstream's experiment, not mine.

3. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOESN'T SAY ABOUT THE FLAG
   Does not touch the upside: 95% / 56% of the bytes with follow-through rising
   (gate 5f-F), all in-region. Does not touch bytes or requests, which are now
   verified invariant at both RTTs. What it says is that
   --readahead-evidence-ratio should not default on for high-latency endpoints
   until the stall mode is understood, and that "bytes and requests are
   byte-identical" is not a sufficient regression gate for it — in-region that
   check misses a fixed +0.35 s, and cross-region it misses a coin-flip between
   4 s and 20 s.

COST, DISCLOSED AGAINST MY OWN ESTIMATE
  I told #256 this would be ~$0.20. It was ~$0.60: 34 cross-region cells at
  851 MB = 28.9 GB of us-west-2 -> us-east-1 egress at $0.02/GB, plus ~$0.02 for
  the server-side copy. The overrun is entirely the extra reps — n=3 produced a
  24 s outlier I could not interpret, so I bought n=8. The throwaway bucket was
  deleted after the traces were banked; re-staging it is one `aws s3 cp`.
