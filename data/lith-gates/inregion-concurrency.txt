GATE 5f-L — THE FOURTH CEILING, BY INTERVENTION RATHER THAN FIT (lith#256)
2026-10-01, $0 (in-region, head node already up)
================================================================================

WHY: upstream derived the 492 from code (`perHandleWindow()` = clamp(budgetBlocks /
openHandles, 2, maxReadahead), budgetBlocks = prefetchBudget / blockSize,
fs.go:1252/:1279) and it checks EXACTLY on my box: MemTotal 33.02 GB x 25%
(mem-cache) x 50% (prefetch-budget) = 4.128 GB; /8 MiB = 492.1 blocks (measured
peak_window 492), /1 MiB = 3937 blocks (so my configured 1024 was delivered in
full, which is why that arm realized its depth and the 8 MiB arm silently did not).
Both my loose ends are filed as #297 (silent cap + log reports configured depth)
and #298 (three bounds disagreeing by 1.5x, smallest winning silently).

They then named a FOURTH ceiling as a guess and explicitly declined to test it:
"--s3-concurrency 128 is a fourth ceiling and depth matters only insofar as it
keeps those 128 slots backlogged — but that's a model through four points, which
is how the last two mechanisms on this issue died. Not going there."

They are right not to FIT it. But it does not have to be fitted: --s3-concurrency
is a flag, so the variable they suspect can be INTERVENED on directly. That is the
lesson already banked from the permutation-null episode — when two explanations
agree on every number you have, perturb the data rather than collect more of it.
In-region, on a box that is already up, this costs $0 and no cross-region egress,
and it lands inside the regime they say they will develop in.

SETUP  in-region (2.2 ms), same 3,776,834,855 B object, dd, GATE OFF, fresh cold
  mount + unique port per cell, n=3. Two configs x three concurrency levels:
    A = --block-size 8MiB  --max-readahead 223   (the shipping default)
    B = --block-size 1MiB  --max-readahead 1024  (the 1.58x cross-region winner)
    --s3-concurrency in {32, 128 (default), 512}
  Realized depth verified from peak_window in every cell.

PRE-REGISTERED, WRITTEN AND COMMITTED BEFORE THE RUN
  H_conc (upstream's guess): the binding resource is concurrent REQUEST SLOTS, and
    a unit only matters because it sets bytes-per-slot. Then:
      - at concurrency 32 the 1 MiB advantage VANISHES OR INVERTS, because 32 slots
        x 1 MiB = 34 MB in flight against 32 x 8 MiB = 268 MB;
      - raising 128 -> 512 helps B (depth 1024 can backlog 512 slots) and barely
        helps A (depth 223 becomes the binding term below 512);
      - so B/A throughput ratio should be MONOTONE INCREASING in concurrency.
  H_unit: the 1 MiB advantage is a property of the unit and survives at every
    concurrency, so B/A is roughly FLAT in concurrency.

  These differ in sign at concurrency 32, which is the cheap decisive cell. If
  H_conc fires, the actionable knob is --s3-concurrency (or bytes-per-slot), NOT
  --block-size, and upstream's step 3 ("the default question, which I want to be
  slow about") is asking about the wrong default.

RESULT 0 — THE INTERVENTION IS CLEAN
  Realized peak_window is 223 in all three A cells and 1024 in all three B cells,
  so --s3-concurrency does not move the depth. Concurrency is an independent knob
  here and the comparison is not confounded by the ceiling upstream just derived.

RESULT 1 — H_conc FIRES ON ITS OWN DECISIVE CELL AND FAILS ON ITS MONOTONICITY
  n=3, median of 3, 3776.835 MB / median s:

  s3-conc   A = 8MiB/223        B = 1MiB/1024       B/A      separation
     32     2.629 s  1437 MB/s  4.097 s   922 MB/s  0.642    9/9, B SLOWER
    128     2.742 s  1377 MB/s  2.317 s  1630 MB/s  1.183    9/9, B FASTER
    512     2.978 s  1268 MB/s  3.014 s  1253 MB/s  0.988    overlap, null

  walls:
    A-c32   2.526 2.629 3.451      B-c32   3.768 4.097 5.158
    A-c128  2.646 2.742 2.836      B-c128  2.314 2.317 2.318
    A-c512  2.721 2.978 3.036      B-c512  2.941 3.014 3.531

  THE SIGN INVERTS. At 32 slots the 1 MiB config is 1.56x SLOWER than the default,
  with complete separation (all 3 B cells above all 3 A cells). That is exactly
  H_conc's signature and exactly what H_unit forbids: 32 x 1 MiB = 34 MB in flight
  against 32 x 8 MiB = 268 MB, and the bigger unit wins when slots are scarce.

  But H_conc's monotonicity prediction fails. It said B/A should keep rising with
  concurrency because depth 1024 can backlog 512 slots while depth 223 cannot.
  Measured B/A is 0.642 -> 1.183 -> 0.988: NOT monotone. Raising 128 -> 512 makes
  BOTH configs slower (A 1377 -> 1268, B 1630 -> 1253) and erases the advantage.

RESULT 2 — WHAT THE INTERVENTION ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES
  The 1 MiB win is NOT a property of the unit. It is a property of the unit x
  concurrency pairing — bytes per slot — and in-region it exists only in a band
  around the shipping default of 128. Below the band the small unit is a clear
  loss; above it, extra slots cost both configs and the advantage is gone.

  Corollary nobody asked for: the SHIPPING default (A) is flat-to-declining in
  concurrency in-region — 1437 at c32, 1377 at c128, 1268 at c512, best at the
  LOWEST setting tested. At 8 MiB in-region the pipe is already full at 32 slots
  and the default 128 is costing ~4%.

RESULT 3 — WHY THIS MATTERS FOR UPSTREAM'S STEP 3
  They wrote that the default question is the one they want to be slow about,
  because --block-size is the fill unit and feeds coalescing, --parts-max, the
  bgzf plane and the footer planes. This adds a reason that is upstream of all of
  those: shrinking the default block size would be a change whose benefit is
  CONTINGENT ON --s3-concurrency STAYING AT ITS DEFAULT, and which actively harms
  anyone who has lowered concurrency — small instances, shared endpoints, or
  politeness to S3. The tunable that actually orders these points is bytes per
  slot, not block size alone, and a default change on one axis of a product is
  the kind of change that looks free until someone moves the other axis.

LIMITS, STATED RATHER THAN BURIED
  - IN-REGION ONLY, 2.2 ms. The 1.58x headline was CROSS-REGION, where the same
    pairing measured 1.18x in-region. More RTT means more latency to hide, so the
    concurrency optimum can move and the c512 null in particular may not hold at
    58.6 ms. I am NOT extrapolating this to the cross-region case; the cell is
    unmeasured and I say so.
  - n=3. Two of the three cells are completely separated (9/9), the third is a
    null with overlap, so the SIGNS are solid and the magnitudes are soft.
  - One object, one access shape (contiguous dd), one box, one open handle.

  Upstream explicitly said they would stop asking for cells they can run
  themselves, I overran their last request by $0.69, and this in-region result

ONE HONEST GAP IN THE ARCHIVE, AND HOW IT IS COVERED
