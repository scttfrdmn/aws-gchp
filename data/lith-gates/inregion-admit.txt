GATE 5f-O — does byte admission hold #55 on the shape where the divisor was load-bearing?
lith#301 / #306 (merged ceeb2b7) / #308.  Head node, in-region, 2026-10-01.

WHY THIS ARM, IN UPSTREAM'S OWN WORDS
  They merged byte admission -- BlockStore.admitCommitted reserves a chunk's bytes against
  --prefetch-budget with a CAS, refuses what will not fit, and perHandleWindow stops
  dividing by the open-descriptor count -- and then asked for exactly one thing back:

    "Re-run arm A against ceeb2b7. 16 concurrent readers, 60 GB over your 8.256 GB tier,
     the shape where admission must not regress #55. [...] every check I have on the
     safety half is weaker than that arm: my fake-server sweep runs at 2.4-3.5x
     production memory pressure, in a direction the network can't produce; my own
     concurrent-reader measurement had two of three cells NIC-suppressed;
     TestPrefetchBudgetNoThrash passes, but at the 1:8 budget-to-tier ratio where my
     sweep showed the rationing barely matters at all."

  They also ruled out the obvious fix first, which is why only the charge changed:
  deleting the rationing outright costs 13.8-22.4x wall and 4.2-5.2x the bytes with
  81-92% of prefetch evicted unread (bench/prefetch-divisor, real S3 in-region).

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES ON THIS SHAPE, AND IT IS NOT NOTHING
  At 16 handles the divisor gave each handle clamp(492/16,2,223) = 30 blocks -- an equal
  SHARE, guaranteed, 16 x 30 x 8 MiB = 4.027 GB aggregate. Admission gives each handle the
  full 223 and caps the POOL at 4.128 GB. The realized totals agree to 2.4%, which is the
  arithmetic I gave upstream and which decided the merge, but the allocation discipline is
  different in kind: equal share vs first-come race. So this gate is not only "does the
  total still fit" -- it is also "does a global pool starve anyone", which is a question
  nobody has asked yet and which the divisor could not have.

ARMS  16 concurrent readers, 16 distinct GEOSFP A3dyn objects, 3 reps, fresh cold mount
      per cell, default flags unless stated.  scripts/lith/gate-admit.sh
  OLD  52138ee   the divisor + the #303/#304 gauges
  NEW  7a8b38e   byte admission (ceeb2b7, plus #308 docs)
  POS  7a8b38e   --prefetch-budget 30GB            POSITIVE CONTROL, see below

  Working set: 60.077 GB = 57,301 chunks of 1 MiB, against a 8.256 GB mem-cache tier
  = 7.28x oversubscribed.  Box: 33.022636032 GB MemTotal, --mem-cache 25% = 8.255659008 GB,
  --prefetch-budget 50% of that = 4.127829504 GB = 492.08 blocks at 8 MiB; maxReadahead
  auto-resolves to 223.

  POS exists because TRAP 1 IS MINE AND IT APPLIES TO ME. 5f-M arm A saturated this box's
  NIC at ~14 Gbps (aggregate 1290 -> 1769 MB/s across N). A NIC ceiling throttles DISPATCH,
  which SUPPRESSES thrash, so a null in NEW is worth having only if this box can show
  thrash at all. POS raises the admission cap to 30 GB -- above the entire 8.256 GB cache
  tier -- so prefetched-unread chunks MUST be evicted if dispatch can ever outrun
  consumption here. A null in POS does not acquit NEW; it bounds NEW, and gets reported
  that way rather than quietly strengthening the result.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS  (committed before the decisive cells ran)

  P1 WINDOW, integer-exact.  OLD 30, NEW 223, POS 223 -- the regime change made visible
     in the #302 gauge. If NEW does not read 223 the merge did not take effect and
     nothing below means anything.

  P2 ADMISSION MUST BIND, i.e. the VALIDITY CHECK.  NEW attempts 16 x 223 x 8 MiB =
     29.93 GB against a 4.128 GB cap, so lith_prefetch_refused_total must be >> 0.
     IF IT READS 0 THE ARM DID NOT TEST ADMISSION and the null is vacuous. This is the
     counter-form of the defect: refusals are the cap doing its job, and they cost
     nothing (upstream's own distinction -- a refusal costs no GET and no bytes, an
     eviction costs both).

  P3 committed_bytes plateaus at <= 4.127829504e9 in OLD and NEW, and ABOVE the cache
     tier in POS. Peak from the 10 Hz sampler, not the final scrape.

UPSTREAM'S TWO FALSIFIERS, TAKEN AS STATED
  F1 RE-DISPATCH.  issued exceeding (total chunks - the demand-filled prefix). Measured
     two ways: the chunk form (issued <= 57,301 - demand prefix; banked OLD at N=16 is
     issued 57,045 and 57,301 - 57,045 = 256 = exactly 16 objects x 16 demand chunks),
     and the byte form, which needs no prefix estimate: lith_s3_bytes_total must stay at
     60.077 GB +-2%. Any chunk fetched twice shows up in both.
  F2 EVICTION.  lith_prefetch_evicted_unread_total rising off its floor. Floor is 0 in
     18/18 banked cells, so ANY non-zero value in NEW is the signal.

MY OWN FALSIFIER, WHICH UPSTREAM DID NOT NAME
  F3 STARVATION.  The divisor rationed by equal share; admission rations by race, and a
     fast handle may take 223 blocks while another gets refused down to nothing. Thrash
     is not the only way a global pool can be worse than a share. Measured as the
     per-reader wall spread within a cell, max/min over the 16 readers.
     THRESHOLD, set in advance: NEW's spread ratio within 1.3x of OLD's is a pass;
     wider than that is a real regression even if F1 and F2 are both clean, and I will
     report it as one. Secondary: lith_prefetch_deestablished_total and
     lith_prefetch_window_halved_total, since a refused prefix could in principle drop a
     handle out of sequential detection.

  P4 WALL.  Banked arm A at N=16 is NIC-bound, so I predict PARITY, NEW/OLD in 0.90-1.10
     on aggregate wall, with ~34 s per cell (60,077 MB at ~1769 MB/s). A large NEW win
     here would be suspicious rather than welcome: the win from this change lives on the
     IDLE-descriptor shape (5f-N, 6-10x), not here, and the whole argument for the merge
     was that it COINCIDES with the divisor on this shape.

LIMITS, STATED UP FRONT
  - One box, one object family, one access shape (whole-object sequential streaming via
    dd at 1 MiB). netCDF discontiguity is absent by construction; that is 5f-G/5f-I
    territory and is not what upstream asked for.
  - 16 readers is the most this 16-core box can run without CPU contending with the
    measurement.
  - POS changes a flag, not the code path, so it tests whether EVICTION is reachable on
    this box -- not whether some other box would thrash at the default.
  - #307 (a --mem-cache below 64 MiB silently disables the memory tier) does not touch
    any arm here: all three run the 8.256 GB default. Flagged because a future
    constrained cell would hit it.

================================================================================
MID-GATE PRE-REGISTRATION, written after rep 1 and BEFORE looking at rep 2
================================================================================
Rep 1 produced a second mechanism I did not anticipate, so the derived prediction below
is a RETRODICTION on rep 1 and is tested OUT OF SAMPLE on rep 2, which is already running
and which I have not looked at. Committed before looking.

  Verified in code, not inferred: internal/fuse/fs.go:1075 Release() deletes the handle
  and records prefetch stats, and calls nothing that releases committed bytes. The only
  dropPrefetched callers are blockstore.go:492 (evict), :910 and :944 (consume / failed
  fill). So commitment is released on CONSUME, EVICTION or FAILED FILL -- never on handle
  close. A handle that closes with outstanding prefetched-unread chunks leaves those bytes
  charged against the mount-wide admission cap, and if the working set fits the memory
  tier, eviction never fires and the charge never clears.

  H_WEDGE: the pool wedges -- committed pinned at the cap with admission permanently
  refusing -- exactly when BOTH hold:
      (a) total working set <= --mem-cache (8.256 GB), so eviction never reclaims, AND
      (b) COVERED < N, so there is someone to starve.
  Tier: 8.255659008 GB. Working sets: L2 4 GiB, L3 6 GiB, L4 8 GiB, L8 16 GiB,
  C 60 GB, D 60 GB, W 62 GB.  COVERED: 2.21 on the --nic-gbps 50 arms, 14.91 on D and W.

  POINT PREDICTION FOR REP 2, all seven arms:
      WEDGE in L3 and L4 only.  L2 exempt by (b) -- COVERED 2.21 >= 2, refused must be 0.
      L8, C, W exempt by (a) -- working set over the tier, so eviction releases.
      D exempt by both.
  Measured as the fraction of sampled instants with committed > open_handles x
  window_commit_bytes, which is a CONSERVATION violation and needs no wall time: one
  handle cannot legitimately commit more than one window. Rep 1: L3 92%, L4 91%, W 4%,
  everything else 0%.

  REFUTER: a wedge in L8, C or W at >10% of samples kills H_WEDGE -- those working sets
  are 1.9-7.5x the tier, so if they wedge too, close-time residue is not what is filling
  the pool and some other charge is.

MECHANISM 1 — THE COVERAGE RATIO, WHICH IS ARITHMETIC ON TWO NUMBERS #308 ALREADY PRINTS
  Admission is a prefix: blockstore.go takes chunks in order and the FIRST refusal ends
  the block. So a handle that cannot fit a full window gets NO prefetch, not a shallower
  one. The divisor sized every handle to budget/N and covered all N shallowly; admission
  covers floor(budget/window_commit) of them fully and starves the rest to synchronous
  1 MiB demand reads.

      COVERED = prefetch_budget_bytes / window_commit_bytes
      covered fraction = min(1, COVERED/N)

  Both terms are in the mount's own new log line:
      "prefetch bounds" window_blocks=223 window_commit_bytes=1870659584
                        prefetch_budget_bytes=4127829504 binding="--inflight-bytes"
  4127829504/1870659584 = 2.21. The budget admits TWO full windows. At 16 readers, 14 get
  nothing, and the log says binding="--inflight-bytes" without mentioning it.

  Predicted vs measured covered fraction, 7 arms, no fitted parameters: 13.8/17.5,
  27.6/35.7, 48.1/50.4, 55.2/63.7, 73.6/79.8, 93.2/94.5, 100/100. Measured runs 1-8 pp
  above predicted in every arm -- the model is a floor, as it should be, since a covered
  reader's commitment is released as it consumes and partially re-admitted by others.
  A starved reader gets 19-32 MB/s where OLD's aggregate is 1,200-1,800 MB/s: ~2% of the
  available bandwidth, which is single-stream synchronous 1 MiB GET latency and nothing
  else. GETs/chunk confirms it: OLD coalesces 7.75 chunks per GET, NEW 1.15.

  THE KNEE IS EXACTLY AT COVERED. L2 (N=2 <= 2.21) is identical to OLD in every counter
  -- issued 7,135 both, refused 0, 925 GETs both -- and 0.89x the wall. L3 (N=3 > 2.21)
  is 10.58x slower. Two readers nothing, three readers catastrophic.

  AND IT IS NOT MY FLAG. Every banked cell of this campaign sets --nic-gbps 50, which
  inflates maxReadahead from this box's default 33 to 223 and so inflates the quantum
  6.8x. So I ran the default: imds-estimate gives 7.5 Gbps, window 33,
  window_commit_bytes 276,824,064, COVERED 14.91. Arm D (N=16 <= 14.91, near parity) is
  0.92x -- NEW slightly FASTER, the predicted null. Arm W sets no bandwidth flag at all
  and simply adds readers to 31 > 14.91: the regression returns at 2.61x. The failure
  condition is a ratio, not a flag, and it is reachable at the shipping default by
  opening enough concurrent readers.

MECHANISM 2 — COMMITMENT IS NEVER RELEASED AT HANDLE CLOSE, SO THE POOL RATCHETS
  Found because one thing in rep 1 did not fit mechanism 1: at L3 the two covered readers
  finish at 4.9 s, which should free the pool, yet the third still takes 64 s. The sampler
  says why. With ONE open handle on the mount, committed climbs to 4,127.3 MB and sits
  pinned at the cap for the last 36 of 64 s. One handle's legitimate maximum is
  window_commit_bytes = 1,870.7 MB. It is holding 2.21x that.

  Verified in code rather than inferred: Release() (internal/fuse/fs.go:1075) deletes the
  handle and records prefetch stats and calls nothing that releases committed bytes. The
  only dropPrefetched callers are blockstore.go:492 (evict), :910 and :944 (consume /
  failed fill). Close is none of those. So a handle that closes with outstanding
  prefetched-unread chunks leaves those bytes charged indefinitely, and if the working set
  fits the memory tier, eviction never fires and the charge never clears.

  This is a CONSERVATION violation and needs no wall time to detect:
      committed > open_handles x window_commit_bytes   is impossible by design.

  H_WEDGE was pre-registered on rep 1 and tested on rep 2 (above, commit cef4b1c).
  Fraction of sampled instants violating conservation, rep 2:
      L3 92% (worst 2.21x)   L4 91% (2.21x)   L2 59% (1.85x)
      W   3% (worst 14.91x)  L8 2%   C 0%   D 0%
  6 of 7 as predicted, and THE MISS IS THE USEFUL PART. I predicted L2 exempt because
  COVERED >= N leaves nobody to starve. L2 has no starvation and no wall cost (0.89x) --
  and it has the leak anyway, 59% of samples. So clause (b) of H_WEDGE was wrong and the
  two things separate cleanly:

      THE LEAK occurs iff working set <= --mem-cache, i.e. iff eviction never reclaims.
        L2 4 GiB, L3 6 GiB, L4 8 GiB -> 59/92/91%.  L8 16 GiB, C 60 GB, D 60 GB,
        W 62 GB -> 0-3%.  7/7 on that rule alone.
      THE HARM additionally needs COVERED < N. L2 has the leak and is harmless; L3/L4
        have the leak and are 10.6x/11.0x.

  The worst single instance is in W, at the shipping default: ONE handle holding the
  entire 4.128 GB pool against a 276.8 MB window commitment, 14.91x. That ratio is
  COVERED again, necessarily -- one handle can hold at most the whole pool, which is
  COVERED of its own windows -- so the same number governs both mechanisms.

  WHY THIS IS WORSE THAN RATIONING, for a real workload: the ratchet is driven by the
  number of handles OPENED OVER TIME, not by how many are open at once. A netCDF reader
  that opens and closes thousands of files, each with readahead outstanding at close,
  charges residue every time. The divisor's bug THROTTLED; this one WEDGES.

POSITIVE CONTROL — THE BOX CAN THRASH, SO THE NULL IN F2 IS REAL
  POS (--prefetch-budget 30GB, cap above the whole 8.256 GB tier) at N=16:
  evicted_unread 46,406 against a floor of 0, used/issued 2,766/57,045 = 4.85%
  follow-through, and 374.63 GB fetched to deliver 43.76 GB of reads = 8.56x
  amplification. 46,406/57,045 = 81.3% of prefetch evicted unread, which lands inside
  upstream's own 81-92% from bench/prefetch-divisor -- their result reproduced on real S3
  from the opposite direction, by loosening the cap instead of deleting the rationing.
  Trap 1 is retired for this box: a null in F2 is not NIC-manufactured, because the same
  box and the same 16 readers produce 46,406 evictions when the cap is loosened.
  DISCLOSURE: I terminated POS's readers at 902 s with 72.8% of the reads delivered. The
  control had already fired and the remaining arms were the decisive ones; its wall is
  therefore a lower bound and is not used in any comparison.

AND THE POSITIVE CONTROL BREAKS F1 AS UPSTREAM STATED IT
  In the real thrash regime, issued PLATEAUS at exactly 57,045 -- the same value as the
  clean OLD run -- because an evicted chunk is re-read as an UNCOVERED DEMAND read, not
  as a second prefetch. uncovered goes to 300,239 and s3_bytes to 8.56x, and issued never
  moves. So "issued exceeding total-chunks-minus-the-demand-prefix" cannot detect
  re-dispatch in the regime it was written for. The byte form (s3_bytes vs working set)
  detects it immediately. I had flagged the byte form as preferable because it needs no
  prefix estimate; the control shows the chunk form is not merely awkward but blind.

WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND IT IS THE LOAD-BEARING THING
  The argument that decided this merge was mine: "candidate 3 is a strict generalization
  -- it coincides with the divisor where the divisor is right (16 concurrent readers,
  4.027 vs 4.128 GB, within 2.4%) and fixes it where it is wrong (idle handles, 241x)."
  Upstream said so explicitly: "your 'strict generalization' framing is what decided it."

  The 2.4% is true and it is the wrong comparison. It compares AGGREGATE TOTALS, and what
  the divisor was providing was an ALLOCATION DISCIPLINE: 16 x 30 blocks covers sixteen
  readers shallowly; 2 x 223 + 14 x 0 commits the same total and covers two. Equal totals,
  opposite outcomes. I checked that the budget still fits and never asked how it is
  distributed, and "strict generalization" was exactly the wrong frame -- admission is not
  a superset of the divisor's behaviour, it is a different allocator that happens to agree
  on one scalar.

  Candidate 1 ("don't charge idle handles"), which upstream ruled out on my Result 3 and
  which I called a no-op on this shape, is now the one indicated: keep the division --
  that is the share, and the share is what was load-bearing -- and fix only the divisor's
  input, counting established sequential streams instead of open descriptors. My Result 3
  showed the divisor conserves the RAM fraction; it did not show the PROXY had to go.

COST  $0.27 of S3 GET requests (679,013 GETs, in-region so no transfer), on a head node
  that was already running. The expensive cells are the starved ones -- a starved reader
  issues ~1 GET per MiB where a covered one issues 1 per 8 MiB -- so the regression paid
