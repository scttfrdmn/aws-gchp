GATE 5f-O — does byte admission hold #55 on the shape where the divisor was load-bearing?
lith#301 / #306 (merged ceeb2b7) / #308.  Head node, in-region, 2026-10-01.

WHY THIS ARM, IN UPSTREAM'S OWN WORDS
  They merged byte admission -- BlockStore.admitCommitted reserves a chunk's bytes against
  --prefetch-budget with a CAS, refuses what will not fit, and perHandleWindow stops
  dividing by the open-descriptor count -- and then asked for exactly one thing back:

    "Re-run arm A against ceeb2b7. 16 concurrent readers, 60 GB over your 8.256 GB tier,
     the shape where admission must not regress #55. [...] every check I have on the
     safety half is weaker than that arm: my fake-server sweep runs at 2.4-3.5x
     production memory pressure, in a direction the network can't produce; my own
     concurrent-reader measurement had two of three cells NIC-suppressed;
     TestPrefetchBudgetNoThrash passes, but at the 1:8 budget-to-tier ratio where my
     sweep showed the rationing barely matters at all."

  They also ruled out the obvious fix first, which is why only the charge changed:
  deleting the rationing outright costs 13.8-22.4x wall and 4.2-5.2x the bytes with
  81-92% of prefetch evicted unread (bench/prefetch-divisor, real S3 in-region).

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES ON THIS SHAPE, AND IT IS NOT NOTHING
  At 16 handles the divisor gave each handle clamp(492/16,2,223) = 30 blocks -- an equal
  SHARE, guaranteed, 16 x 30 x 8 MiB = 4.027 GB aggregate. Admission gives each handle the
  full 223 and caps the POOL at 4.128 GB. The realized totals agree to 2.4%, which is the
  arithmetic I gave upstream and which decided the merge, but the allocation discipline is
  different in kind: equal share vs first-come race. So this gate is not only "does the
  total still fit" -- it is also "does a global pool starve anyone", which is a question
  nobody has asked yet and which the divisor could not have.

ARMS  16 concurrent readers, 16 distinct GEOSFP A3dyn objects, 3 reps, fresh cold mount
      per cell, default flags unless stated.  scripts/lith/gate-admit.sh
  OLD  52138ee   the divisor + the #303/#304 gauges
  NEW  7a8b38e   byte admission (ceeb2b7, plus #308 docs)
  POS  7a8b38e   --prefetch-budget 30GB            POSITIVE CONTROL, see below

  Working set: 60.077 GB = 57,301 chunks of 1 MiB, against a 8.256 GB mem-cache tier
  = 7.28x oversubscribed.  Box: 33.022636032 GB MemTotal, --mem-cache 25% = 8.255659008 GB,
  --prefetch-budget 50% of that = 4.127829504 GB = 492.08 blocks at 8 MiB; maxReadahead
  auto-resolves to 223.

  POS exists because TRAP 1 IS MINE AND IT APPLIES TO ME. 5f-M arm A saturated this box's
  NIC at ~14 Gbps (aggregate 1290 -> 1769 MB/s across N). A NIC ceiling throttles DISPATCH,
  which SUPPRESSES thrash, so a null in NEW is worth having only if this box can show
  thrash at all. POS raises the admission cap to 30 GB -- above the entire 8.256 GB cache
  tier -- so prefetched-unread chunks MUST be evicted if dispatch can ever outrun
  consumption here. A null in POS does not acquit NEW; it bounds NEW, and gets reported
  that way rather than quietly strengthening the result.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS  (committed before the decisive cells ran)

  P1 WINDOW, integer-exact.  OLD 30, NEW 223, POS 223 -- the regime change made visible
     in the #302 gauge. If NEW does not read 223 the merge did not take effect and
     nothing below means anything.

  P2 ADMISSION MUST BIND, i.e. the VALIDITY CHECK.  NEW attempts 16 x 223 x 8 MiB =
     29.93 GB against a 4.128 GB cap, so lith_prefetch_refused_total must be >> 0.
     IF IT READS 0 THE ARM DID NOT TEST ADMISSION and the null is vacuous. This is the
     counter-form of the defect: refusals are the cap doing its job, and they cost
     nothing (upstream's own distinction -- a refusal costs no GET and no bytes, an
     eviction costs both).

  P3 committed_bytes plateaus at <= 4.127829504e9 in OLD and NEW, and ABOVE the cache
     tier in POS. Peak from the 10 Hz sampler, not the final scrape.

UPSTREAM'S TWO FALSIFIERS, TAKEN AS STATED
  F1 RE-DISPATCH.  issued exceeding (total chunks - the demand-filled prefix). Measured
     two ways: the chunk form (issued <= 57,301 - demand prefix; banked OLD at N=16 is
     issued 57,045 and 57,301 - 57,045 = 256 = exactly 16 objects x 16 demand chunks),
     and the byte form, which needs no prefix estimate: lith_s3_bytes_total must stay at
     60.077 GB +-2%. Any chunk fetched twice shows up in both.
  F2 EVICTION.  lith_prefetch_evicted_unread_total rising off its floor. Floor is 0 in
     18/18 banked cells, so ANY non-zero value in NEW is the signal.

MY OWN FALSIFIER, WHICH UPSTREAM DID NOT NAME
  F3 STARVATION.  The divisor rationed by equal share; admission rations by race, and a
     fast handle may take 223 blocks while another gets refused down to nothing. Thrash
     is not the only way a global pool can be worse than a share. Measured as the
     per-reader wall spread within a cell, max/min over the 16 readers.
     THRESHOLD, set in advance: NEW's spread ratio within 1.3x of OLD's is a pass;
     wider than that is a real regression even if F1 and F2 are both clean, and I will
     report it as one. Secondary: lith_prefetch_deestablished_total and
     lith_prefetch_window_halved_total, since a refused prefix could in principle drop a
     handle out of sequential detection.

  P4 WALL.  Banked arm A at N=16 is NIC-bound, so I predict PARITY, NEW/OLD in 0.90-1.10
     on aggregate wall, with ~34 s per cell (60,077 MB at ~1769 MB/s). A large NEW win
     here would be suspicious rather than welcome: the win from this change lives on the
     IDLE-descriptor shape (5f-N, 6-10x), not here, and the whole argument for the merge
     was that it COINCIDES with the divisor on this shape.

LIMITS, STATED UP FRONT
  - One box, one object family, one access shape (whole-object sequential streaming via
    dd at 1 MiB). netCDF discontiguity is absent by construction; that is 5f-G/5f-I
    territory and is not what upstream asked for.
  - 16 readers is the most this 16-core box can run without CPU contending with the
    measurement.
  - POS changes a flag, not the code path, so it tests whether EVICTION is reachable on
    this box -- not whether some other box would thrash at the default.
  - #307 (a --mem-cache below 64 MiB silently disables the memory tier) does not touch
    any arm here: all three run the 8.256 GB default. Flagged because a future
    constrained cell would hit it.

================================================================================
MID-GATE PRE-REGISTRATION, written after rep 1 and BEFORE looking at rep 2
================================================================================
Rep 1 produced a second mechanism I did not anticipate, so the derived prediction below
is a RETRODICTION on rep 1 and is tested OUT OF SAMPLE on rep 2, which is already running
and which I have not looked at. Committed before looking.

  Verified in code, not inferred: internal/fuse/fs.go:1075 Release() deletes the handle
  and records prefetch stats, and calls nothing that releases committed bytes. The only
  dropPrefetched callers are blockstore.go:492 (evict), :910 and :944 (consume / failed
  fill). So commitment is released on CONSUME, EVICTION or FAILED FILL -- never on handle
  close. A handle that closes with outstanding prefetched-unread chunks leaves those bytes
  charged against the mount-wide admission cap, and if the working set fits the memory
  tier, eviction never fires and the charge never clears.

  H_WEDGE: the pool wedges -- committed pinned at the cap with admission permanently
  refusing -- exactly when BOTH hold:
      (a) total working set <= --mem-cache (8.256 GB), so eviction never reclaims, AND
      (b) COVERED < N, so there is someone to starve.
  Tier: 8.255659008 GB. Working sets: L2 4 GiB, L3 6 GiB, L4 8 GiB, L8 16 GiB,
  C 60 GB, D 60 GB, W 62 GB.  COVERED: 2.21 on the --nic-gbps 50 arms, 14.91 on D and W.

  POINT PREDICTION FOR REP 2, all seven arms:
      WEDGE in L3 and L4 only.  L2 exempt by (b) -- COVERED 2.21 >= 2, refused must be 0.
      L8, C, W exempt by (a) -- working set over the tier, so eviction releases.
      D exempt by both.
  Measured as the fraction of sampled instants with committed > open_handles x
  window_commit_bytes, which is a CONSERVATION violation and needs no wall time: one
  handle cannot legitimately commit more than one window. Rep 1: L3 92%, L4 91%, W 4%,
  everything else 0%.

  REFUTER: a wedge in L8, C or W at >10% of samples kills H_WEDGE -- those working sets
  are 1.9-7.5x the tier, so if they wedge too, close-time residue is not what is filling
  the pool and some other charge is.
