GATE 5f-G — IS THE RAMP COST A PROPERTY OF THE FLAG OR OF THE READER? (lith#256)
2026-09-30, $0 (head node only)
================================================================================

WHY: I reported --readahead-evidence-ratio 4 costing +22.3% GETs on a long
sequential read. Upstream could not reproduce it: a pure `cat` stream over a
1161 MiB object came back BIT-IDENTICAL in bytes and requests at ratio 0 vs 4.
Their explanation was that netCDF4 walks the object variable by variable, so
accrued evidence repeatedly lags the window and the ramp is paid more than once,
while a contiguous stream never binds the evidence bound at all. Their question
back was the right one: does the +22.3% hold if the reader streams, on MY box
and the SAME object, with no confound of a different file or a different NIC?

SETUP  same two objects as gates 5f-E/5f-F, same lith dev build
  (v1.1.3-15-gb3d78d8), --nic-gbps 50, fresh mount + unique metrics port per cell.

    hco  s3://gcgrid/HEMCO/GT_Chlorine/v2024-05/GT_Chlorine_01_01_2000_V1.0.0.nc
  five readers, each over the whole object at both ratios:
    dd      one contiguous stream, 1 MiB reads          (3 reps)

    cat     `cat` itself                                (4 reps)  = upstream's exact case
    pread   python os.pread, 1 MiB, increasing offsets  (3 reps)  contiguous, but
                                                                  NOT the netCDF4 process
    nc      every data variable end to end via netCDF4  (3 reps)  met 5 vars, hco 12

1. UPSTREAM'S READER EXPLANATION IS CONFIRMED, ON MY BOX, WITH MY OBJECTS
   Every contiguous reader is +0.0% in requests and 0.00% in bytes at ratio 4,
   including `cat` at its own 128 KiB read size. So READ SIZE IS NOT THE AXIS:
   dd@1M, dd@128K and cat all agree at zero. The only arms that pay requests are
   the netCDF4 arms, and they pay in proportion to DISCONTIGUITY:
        1 stream      -> +0.0%   (dd, dd128, cat, pread)
        5 variables   -> +0.6%   (met/nc)
       12 variables   -> +21.4%  (hco/nc)
   The evidence bound only bills a reader that makes it re-earn the window. My
   +22.3% was real but it was never a property of the flag; it was a property of
   the reader, exactly as upstream said.

2. CORRECTION TO MY OWN PUBLISHED FIGURE: +22.3% -> +21.4%
   That number came from n=1. With 3 reps, hco/nc r0 = {130,131,131} and
   r4 = {159,159,159}, so the median-to-median cost is +21.4%. The figure in
   data/lith-gates/second-workload.txt was the low end of r0, not its centre.

3. WHAT UPSTREAM'S COUNTERS COULD NOT SEE: THE RAMP HAS A WALL COST
   On all eight streaming cells the ramp is free in every counter and costs
   +0.24 to +0.49 s of wall (+17% to +42%). Paired by rep, ratio 4 is slower in
   27 of 34 pairs; the 7 exceptions are all reps whose ratio-0 wall is that arm's
   OWN MAXIMUM (2.10, 1.81, 1.66, 3.56 s — cold-page/VM warmup), i.e. ratio 0
   outliers rather than ratio 4 wins. By arm median, 8 of 8 streaming arms are
   slower at ratio 4 with zero variance in bytes or requests.
   The delta is roughly FIXED in seconds (mean +0.355 s, range 0.24-0.49) rather
   than proportional, which is what a one-time ramp should look like: the window
   starts at the floor and grows, so fewer blocks are in flight early and the
   fixed cost is latency, not work. That is why it is 17-42% of a 1-2 s streaming
   read and 0-6% of a 14-29 s decompression-bound read.
   => "bytes and requests are byte-identical" is a true and insufficient
      regression check for this flag. Request COUNT is conserved; request
      CONCURRENCY during the ramp is not, and only wall clock shows it.

4. AN INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION OF UPSTREAM'S REP-1 DISCARD
   Upstream discarded their first rep as a warmup. Unprompted and on different
   hardware, my rep 1 is the high outlier on both 3-rep dd arms (met 2.10 vs
   1.29/1.38; hco 1.81 vs 1.02/1.03). Same artifact, same remedy.

READING THE TWO GATES TOGETHER
  5f-F priced the flag as "cheap on the sequential side, 95% of the bytes saved
  on the low-coverage side". 5f-G narrows the first half: on a genuinely
  contiguous reader the flag is free in bytes and requests but costs a fixed
  ~0.35 s of ramp latency; on a discontiguous reader it costs up to +21% of
  requests as well. Neither cost touches the cold-start floor (gate 5f-F
  channel B, bit-identical). The flag is still cheap relative to what it saves,
  but the price tag has a term in it that counters do not carry.
