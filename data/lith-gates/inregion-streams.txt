WHAT UPSTREAM ASKED FOR
================================================================================

5f-O measured byte-exact admission (ceeb2b7) at 5.66x slower on the arm they asked for and
up to 11.03x elsewhere. They accepted it, reverted it (#309), added floor visibility (#310),
and then repaired the divisor's INPUT rather than removing the division (#311, merged

    clamp(budgetBlocks / N, 2, maxReadahead)
      N was  len(f.handles)            -- open DESCRIPTORS, cross-process, incl. never read
      N is   streamingHandles.Load()   -- handles the detector is prefetching for

maintained by +1/-1 deltas from each read's detector transition and from Release, not by a
scan (perHandleWindow runs on every read; an O(handles) scan at 6229 handles would cost more
than the misallocation it fixes).

Their asks, verbatim in substance:

  1. "Arm A. Prediction: unchanged from your pre-ceeb2b7 baseline on the concurrent-reader
     arms, improved on the production shape. IF THE CONCURRENT ARMS MOVE AT ALL, THE DELTA
     ACCOUNTING IS WRONG -- that is the falsifier, and I'd want it before anything else."
  2. "lith_streaming_handles vs lith_open_handles on a production mount. Expect ~48 against
     ~288. If streams come out near 288, then most of those descriptors establish and idle,
     and the residual is the dominant term rather than a footnote."

And one they volunteered as the thing they'd most want contradicted:

  "N readers starting together each see a near-zero count and so a full window. What bounds
   the burst is the geometric ramp -- a first dispatch is 2 blocks, not 223 -- so the count
   settles long before any window is deep. I believe this is fine; it is the thing I'd most
   want a real measurement to contradict."

Also noted by them, deliberately unbuilt: #312, the handle that establishes, reads a little,
and idles. It keeps its share until close or collapse to Random. They will not build an
idle-out on speculation, because the divisor would then change on every idle and
re-establish, and I already measured eleven dispatch bursts from the divisor merely DROPPING
as handles closed.

================================================================================
THE SEPARATION THIS GATE BUYS, WHICH IS WHY IT IS WORTH RUNNING
================================================================================

5f-M's two held-descriptor arms are, exactly, the two issues:

    arm C   descriptors opened and NEVER READ            = #301, closed, fixed by construction
    arm B   descriptors opened + ONE 4 KiB pread, idle   = #312, open, magnitude unknown

#311 drops never-read descriptors from the divisor as a matter of code. Whether it helps
arm B turns on a question nobody has measured: DOES A SINGLE 4 KiB READ PUT THE DETECTOR IN
prefetch.Sequential? The answer is not guessable from the patch -- establishment is a
contiguity judgment, and one read has no second read to be contiguous with.

  if a 4 KiB pread does NOT establish  ->  arm B is fixed too, #312 is a footnote
  if it DOES establish                 ->  arm B stays at the floor, and the residual is the
                                           DOMINANT term on every netCDF workload, which is
                                           the finding they said would change what they
                                           build next

lith_streaming_handles reads out which branch is true directly, so the gauge decides it and
the wall clock confirms it. Both branches are informative, which is the point: this is not a
gate that can only pass.

MID-GATE PRE-REGISTRATION (5f-P2): THE DISCRIMINATOR, written before rep 2 was read
================================================================================

Rep 1 arm R, the only cell read at the time of writing:

    OLD  33.97 s   per-reader 32.25-33.97  spread 1.05x   evicted_unread 0     GETs 7396
    NEW 106.53 s   per-reader 29.11-106.52 spread 3.66x   evicted_unread 4537  GETs 12275

P1 IS BREACHED: 3.14x, far outside [0.90, 1.10]. P4 IS CONTRADICTED: peak committed
12.9436 GB against a 4.1278 GB budget = 3.14x, where the threshold was 1.5x.

But the cause upstream named is NOT what broke. The delta accounting is CORRECT:
streaming_handles == open_handles == 16 exactly through the whole steady state, and the
realized window is 30 = 492/16, which is the share the divisor is supposed to give. So the
falsifier fired and the diagnosis attached to it ("if the concurrent arms move at all, the
delta accounting is wrong") does not hold. Something else does.

The sampled series says what, at 5 Hz:

    t=6.05   16 streams, window 30,  committed 12.944 GB   <- 3.14x the budget, already
    t=24.20  16 streams, window 30,  committed  4.351 GB
    t=30.26  15 streams, window 32,  committed  2.906 GB
    t=36.33   3 streams, window 164, committed  0.707 GB   <- 13 readers are DONE at ~33 s,
    t=72.65   3 streams, window 164, committed  2.680 GB      the same time OLD took for all 16
    t=78.71   2 streams, window 223, committed  2.611 GB
    t=102.92  1 stream,  window 223, committed  1.787 GB   <- last reader finishes at 106.5 s

So this is not a uniform slowdown, it is a FAIRNESS COLLAPSE: 13 of 16 readers finish on
OLD's schedule and 3 become permanent losers. Two sub-mechanisms are consistent with every
number above, and they have opposite fixes, so guessing between them is exactly what this
project does not do.

  H-BURST   The start transient makes permanent losers. All 16 readers start within ~50 ms,
            each computes its window from a count that does not yet include itself
            (perHandleWindow runs before the Observe that establishes), so each sees N~0 and
            takes 223. 16 x 223 x 8 MiB = 29.9 GB potential against an 8.256 GB memory tier;
            realized peak 12.94 GB, which is already 1.57x the tier. Blocks are therefore
            evicted BEFORE being read (4537 of them), and an evicted-unread block returns as a
            synchronous demand read, which is the 5f-O starvation rate. Whichever readers lose
            that first cache race fall behind, and falling behind makes them lose it again.
            Upstream's bound -- "the geometric ramp is what bounds the burst, a first dispatch
            is 2 blocks not 223" -- bounds the FIRST dispatch and not the ramp's integral:
            16 ramps running together reach 12.9 GB committed within 6 s.

  H-QUEUE   The late window inflation starves the stragglers. As readers finish, N falls, so
            every surviving reader's window GROWS: 30 -> 32 -> 164 -> 223. With 3 readers at
            164 blocks the mount has up to 492 chunk requests outstanding against a fixed
            --s3-concurrency of 128, so the block a reader needs next can queue behind
            hundreds it will not need for seconds. That is 5f-L's bytes-per-slot result in a
            new place. Committed RISING monotonically from 0.71 to 2.68 GB between t=36 and
            t=73 while only 3 readers are live, at an aggregate ~115 MB/s against the 1185
            MB/s this box does with one reader, is the signature.

PRE-REGISTERED DISCRIMINATOR, three cells on NEW, 16 concurrent streamers, 2 reps:

  R-CAP   --max-readahead 30.  Pins every reader at the share 16 readers are entitled to, so
          no burst is possible and no late inflation either.
          H-BURST predicts: wall within 10% of OLD (33-37 s), spread <= 1.2x,
          evicted_unread <= 100, peak committed <= 4.2 GB. This is the cell that would show
          the steady-state share is fine and only the TRANSIENT is wrong.
  R-CONC  --s3-concurrency 512, --max-readahead default.
          H-QUEUE predicts >= 20% faster than NEW baseline. H-BURST predicts no help or
          harm, and 5f-L is the prior: at the 8 MiB unit, 128 -> 512 slowed BOTH arms there.
  R-PB    --prefetch-budget 1GB (budgetBlocks 119, so 119/16 = 7 at steady state and 119 even
          at N=1).  Caps the burst by the NUMERATOR instead of the window. If H-BURST holds,
          this also restores fairness; if it does not restore while R-CAP does, then the
          window ceiling and not the committed total is what matters, which is a third thing
          and I will say so.

INTERPRETATION RULE, fixed now: R-CAP restoring and R-CONC not helping => H-BURST, and the
repair is to clamp a new handle's window by the count it is about to join (budget/(N+1)) or to
refuse to inflate a window on a count DECREASE, neither of which needs a clock in the read
path. R-CONC helping and R-CAP not => H-QUEUE, and the repair is to bound outstanding chunk
requests rather than window depth. Both helping => the two are the same mechanism seen from
two sides, and I will report it as unseparated rather than pick.

What this cannot settle: whether the 3 stragglers are stable identities (the loser set) or
whether the loss rotates. The per-reader walls give the distribution but not the trajectory,
and --pf-trace at 16 readers over 60 GB is ~300 MB of CSV per cell, which is affordable if
the above comes out ambiguous but is not worth it in advance.

RPB restores -- and its peak committed is 4.236 GB against the 1 GB budget it was given, so
it exceeded its OWN budget 4.2x and was fast anyway. P4 asked whether committed stays inside
the BUDGET. That was the wrong denominator. Both restoring arms land at 0.50-0.51x the
8.256 GB memory tier and both collapsing arms land at 1.58x it, across two different knobs
that move the burst by different routes (window ceiling vs numerator). So the quantity that
tracks the collapse is peak committed RELATIVE TO THE CACHE TIER, not relative to the budget,
and the named mechanism is eviction: commit more than the tier holds and blocks are evicted
before their reader arrives (evicted_unread 4537 in RBASE, 0 in OLD), and an evicted-unread
block returns as a synchronous demand read.

That is still only a correlation over four cells, and all four moved the burst. The causal
control is to move the TIER and leave the burst alone, which is possible because
--prefetch-budget is independent of --mem-cache:

  R-TIER   --mem-cache 20GB --prefetch-budget 4.127829504GB
           Budget, budgetBlocks (492), maxReadahead (223) and therefore every window this
           mount can choose are BIT-IDENTICAL to RBASE. The only thing that changes is how
           much the cache can hold: 20 GB against RBASE's realized 13.078 GB peak, so the
           same burst now fits.

PRE-REGISTERED, 2 reps, NEW binary, 16 concurrent streamers, everything else as RBASE:

  H-EVICT (the claim above)   RTIER wall within 15% of OLD's 33.76 s median => 29-39 s,
      spread <= 1.3, evicted_unread <= 100, AND peak committed still >= 10 GB (if committed
      drops, the intervention did not do what it says and the cell is void).
  H-EVICT IS FALSE IF  RTIER wall stays >= 80 s with evicted_unread >= 1000 removed, or
      stays >= 80 s at all while committed stays >= 10 GB. Then the burst costs something
      other than eviction -- in-flight contention that neither 512 slots nor a bigger cache
      relieves -- and I will report the mechanism as UNRESOLVED rather than assert eviction,
      because RCONC already failed to help and that would leave no measured cause standing.
  INTERMEDIATE  39-80 s: eviction is a contributing term and not the whole cost; report the
      fraction recovered, do not round it to a mechanism.

Confound stated in advance: 20 GB of page-cache-backed tier on a 33.0 GB box leaves ~13 GB,
and the readers are dd to /dev/null so they hold nothing. If the cell shows memory pressure
(any swap activity, or the daemon's RSS near 20 GB with the wall degrading) the result is
void and reported as void, not as a null.

Why this is worth the money even though the rule already fired: the repair I offered upstream
(size a new handle's window from budget/(N+1), refuse to inflate on a count DECREASE) follows
from H-BURST either way. But if eviction is the proximate cost, then the sufficient condition
is much simpler to state and to test in CI -- peak committed must stay under the tier, which
is one inequality over two gauges they already export -- and it tells them the bound they
need is on the SUM over handles, not on any one handle's window. That is a different patch
from the one I proposed, so it is worth knowing which.

COST  ~120 GB in-region GETs, ~15k requests, ~$0.006, on the already-running head node.

    MemAvailable floor 0.46 GB   lith RSS peak 30.56 GB   SwapTotal 0

so the walls they printed (24.40 s, 27.50 s, spread 1.000 at three decimals -- all 16 readers
terminating within 2 ms of each other is a daemon death, not a read) are time-to-OOM and are
DISCARDED. This is the confound I wrote down before running, and it is reported as void rather
than as the spectacular result it would otherwise look like.

What the void measures instead, and it is worth more than the cell was:

    lith RSS peak 30.56 GB  ~=  --mem-cache 20 GB  +  peak committed 13.078 GB  =  33.08 GB
    MemTotal                                                                   =  33.02 GB

The configured tier and the in-flight prefetch are ADDITIVE in RSS, to 0.2%. Prefetch bytes
are committed and dispatched without being charged against the block cache they are about to
land in, so a mount's true footprint is --mem-cache PLUS whatever the burst reaches, and
--mem-cache alone does not bound it. On the DEFAULT configuration that is 8.256 + 13.078 =
21.3 GB on this box -- survivable here, but the burst ceiling is 16 x 223 x 8 MiB = 29.9 GB,
so a mount at defaults with enough concurrent readers can exceed system memory with no knob
set wrongly by anyone. That is a different and more serious issue than the fairness collapse,
and it cannot be deduced from the gauges, because committed_bytes is exported but is not
compared to anything that constrains it.

PRE-REGISTERED REPLACEMENT (5f-P2c): the reader-count ladder, which risks no memory at all
written before any ladder cell ran
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The tier cannot be raised above the burst on a 33 GB box at 16 readers, because the two are
additive. So move the BURST under the tier instead by varying the reader count, which needs no
flag and no extra memory: peak committed scales with the number of concurrent readers, the
8.256 GB tier is fixed, and H-EVICT therefore predicts a KNEE where one crosses the other --
a sharper claim than the binary restore the void cell was going to test.

From the measured 13.078 GB at N=16, peak committed per reader is ~0.817 GB, so the crossing
is at N ~= 8.256/0.817 ~= 10.1 readers.

  N=4   predicted committed ~3.3 GB  (0.40x tier)  no eviction, no collapse, spread <= 1.3
  N=8   predicted committed ~6.5 GB  (0.79x tier)  no eviction, no collapse, spread <= 1.3
  N=12  predicted committed ~9.8 GB  (1.19x tier)  eviction BEGINS, spread > 1.5
  N=16  predicted committed ~13.1 GB (1.58x tier)  the known collapse, spread ~3.6

H-EVICT PASSES IFF  evicted_unread == 0 at N=4 and N=8 and > 1000 at N=12 and N=16, AND the
    spread stays <= 1.3 at N<=8 and exceeds 1.5 at N>=12, i.e. the knee sits between 8 and 12
    where the arithmetic puts it and not somewhere else.
H-EVICT IS FALSE IF  the spread is already > 1.5 at N=4 or N=8 with evicted_unread == 0. Then
    concurrency per se degrades fairness without eviction, eviction is a consequence rather
    than the cause, and the mechanism goes back to unresolved.
GRADED OUTCOME  if eviction starts where predicted but the walls degrade earlier or later than
    the eviction does, report the two curves separately. The interesting quantity is which one
    the wall follows.

One cell is added to bound the additive-RSS finding rather than infer it: N=16 with
--mem-cache 15GB --prefetch-budget 4127829504 (15 + 13.1 = 28.1 GB predicted RSS, ~4.9 GB
headroom on this box). It runs LAST, after the ladder is banked, so an OOM there costs nothing
already earned; and if it OOMs too, the additive relation is confirmed a second time at a
different tier, which is the stronger reading of a failure.

COST  4 ladder N values + 1 tier cell, 2 reps, 15-60 GB per cell, ~$0.01 total, same box.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CONDITION, STATED SO IT CAN BE CHECKED WITHOUT A BOX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The transient burst is N readers each taking maxReadahead before the stream count rises, so
the quantity that must fit in the cache is

    N  x  maxReadahead  x  --block-size        vs        --mem-cache

with maxReadahead = 1.5 x (nic_bytes_per_sec x 0.2) / block-size when --max-readahead is auto.
Substituting and cancelling block-size, harm is expected when

    N  >  mem_cache / (0.2 x nic_bytes_per_sec)                        [N_crit]

and at defaults (--mem-cache = RAM/4) that is N_crit = 1.25 x RAM_bytes / nic_bytes_per_sec.
The ladder puts the realized knee at ~2.4x N_crit, because committed is sublinear in N -- not
every reader peaks at once -- so N_crit is conservative by about that factor.

    this box, --nic-gbps 15 (honest)   N_crit = 22    ran 16   clean        <- matches
    this box, --nic-gbps 50 (mine)     N_crit =  6.6  ran 16   collapsed    <- matches
    c8gn.48xlarge  384 GB, 600 Gbps    N_crit =  6.4  at DEFAULTS, 192 vCPU
    m8gn.48xlarge  768 GB, 600 Gbps    N_crit = 12.8  at DEFAULTS, 192 vCPU

THIS IS WHY THE CONDITIONALITY IS NOT A DISMISSAL. I had to overstate my NIC 3.3x to enter the
regime on a 15 Gbps box. A real fat-NIC instance is IN the regime at stock defaults with ~7-13
concurrent readers, because N_crit falls as the NIC grows relative to RAM -- and those are
exactly the instances this project runs GCHP on, with 48-192 ranks. The small box needed a
flag; the big box needs nothing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT I NOW THINK THE REPAIR IS, having been wrong once about the denominator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not "committed <= budget" (RPB violates it 4.2x and is fast). Not "committed <= tier" (N=8
violates it 1.22x and is clean). The transient is the thing, so bound the transient:

  1. Size a new handle's window from the count it is ABOUT TO JOIN, budget/(N+1), since
     perHandleWindow runs before the Observe that establishes the handle. Clock-free.
  2. Refuse to INFLATE a window on a count DECREASE. A reader already mid-stream has no need
     of a deeper window, and this alone removes the measured 30 -> 32 -> 164 -> 223 climb.
  3. The aggregate guard, if one is wanted, is on the SUM of windows against the TIER, not
     against the budget, and it must tolerate 1.2x (N=8 is fine there) -- so ~1.4x tier is the
     latest it can fire and still be ahead of the collapse.
  4. Charge in-flight prefetch against --mem-cache, or document that RSS is tier + burst. The
     20 GB cell OOM-killed a daemon on a 33 GB box with a 20 GB cache setting, which no user
     would predict from the flag's name.

LIMITS, stated rather than discovered later:
- One box, 16 cores, 33 GB, 2.2 ms RTT, in-region. N=16 equals the core count, so reader CPU
  and reader count move together in the ladder; the OLD/NEW comparison controls for it (same
  16 dd processes, only the binary differs) but the LADDER does not.
- The collapse threshold 1.44x-1.58x is bracketed by two ladder rungs, not resolved.
- 5 Hz gauge sampling; a sub-200 ms burst is invisible, so the 13.078 GB peak is a lower bound
  and the true transient may be larger.
- peak_window in the P2 cells includes the t=0 pre-reader sample, where the gauge reads 223
  with nothing open. peak COMMITTED is the burst evidence; peak window is not.

COST  ~$0.03 for 5f-P (estimated $0.02-0.05), plus ~$0.02 for P2/P2b/P2c/P2d, all in-region
GETs on the already-running gchp-lith-ab head node. No egress, no new instance.

Upstream (#313/#301 close-out) asked for the fat-NIC cell at stock defaults and predicted:
"one 192-vCPU box at stock defaults with 8 readers should collapse", from
N_crit = mem_cache / (0.2 x nic_bytes_per_sec) = 6.4 on c8gn.48xlarge.

That formula prices each reader's burst at maxReadahead x blockSize. The code bounds it
twice more, and neither bound appears in N_crit:

  (a) EOF. blockstore.Prefetch returns when c0*ChunkSize >= objSize, so no reader
      commits past its own object.
  (b) Dedup. Prefetch skips a chunk already in bs.prefetched, so N handles on ONE
      object commit that object once. The aggregate is over DISTINCT objects.

So the burst is  sum over distinct streamed objects of min(maxRA x bs, budget, objSize).
On c8gn.48xlarge at stock defaults: maxRA = 1.5 x (75e9 x 0.2)/8 MiB = 2682 blocks
= 22.5 GB, tier ~101 GB. A GEOS-FP 0.25 A3dyn is 3.76 GB, so the EOF bound binds 6x
below the window, and 8 readers have a HARD ceiling of 8 x 3.76 = 30 GB = 0.30x tier.
The proposed cell cannot collapse on GCHP's largest met object; a null there would read
as a refutation of N_crit and would be neither. (Same shape as the GT_Chlorine confound:
an object too small to exercise the mechanism.)
