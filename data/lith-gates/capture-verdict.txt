2. THE PRE-REGISTERED RULE CANNOT BE EVALUATED — AND THE REASON IS THE FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scored with lith-pfreplay from c922cc7, `-issued` taken per class from the live mounts
(met 2944, HEMCO 4680).

At face value the tool returns a verdict:
     mean_abs_gap_blocks   met/a=+0.604  met/b=+0.616   hemco/a=+0.250  hemco/b=+0.199
     AUC(hemco vs met) = 0.670  (n=334 vs 286)
     best |rho| = 0.616;  VERDICT: SEPARATION

But the tool ALSO voids its own output, on every arm:
     state-machine fidelity: ** MISMATCH on 137/598 handles (met) / 178/6272 (hemco) **
                             — the detector replay is wrong; everything below is void.
     denominator sanity (met): 52947.0 MiB of decisions against 5978.9 MiB of distinct
                               objects (8.86x)
       ** IMPLAUSIBLE: a mount cannot usefully dispatch many times an object's size. **
     vs live issued (met): replay 52947 chunk-decisions, mount 2944 chunks fetched
       ** replay is >4x the mount's fetched chunks: too far apart to be dedup alone. **

THE MISMATCH IS CONCENTRATED EXACTLY ON THE HANDLES THAT MATTER:

     class/arm   handles   mismatching   prefetching   prefetching AND faithful
     hemco/a        6272           178           168            4
     hemco/b        6279           175           166            3
     met/a           598           137           143            8
     met/b           603           137           143            8

96% of met's prefetching handles (135/143) have an unfaithful replay. So the rho = 0.604
/ 0.616 that produced "SEPARATION" was computed almost entirely on handles whose labels
the tool itself rejects. Rescoring on only the faithful prefetchers:

     class/arm    n    rho(mean_abs_gap_blocks)   median ft   mean ft
     hemco/a      4            n/a                  0.000     0.0000
     hemco/b      3            n/a                  0.000     0.0000
     met/a        8          -0.412                 0.000     0.0040
     met/b        8          -0.082                 0.000     0.0040
     AUC(hemco vs met) on the faithful subset = 0.438  (n=7 vs 16)

The sign flips between arms and |rho| collapses. Formally |rho| < 0.5 and AUC < 0.7,
which is the letter of NO SEPARATION — but on n=8 and n=3-4 handles, which is precisely
the minimum-n degeneracy flagged in gate 5g and still unguarded. The honest verdict is
therefore NOT "no separation":

     VERDICT: UNEVALUABLE at the fidelity the instrument itself requires. The rule as
     written asks a per-handle question of a workload that has no per-handle locality.


3. WHY: GCHP HAS ~50 HANDLES PER OBJECT, SO THE PER-HANDLE MODEL'S ASSUMPTION FAILS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Straight from the traces (arm a):

     met:    25,666 rows   598 handles   **12 distinct keys**
             mean 49.8 handles per key, max 77
             keys touched by more than one handle: 12/12 = 100%
             handles that are the sole reader of every key they touch: **0 / 598**

     hemco:  33,870 rows  6,272 handles   204 distinct keys
             mean 30.7 handles per key, max 76
             keys touched by more than one handle: 196/204 = 96%
             sole readers: 8 / 6,272
             of the 164 mismatching prefetchers, sole readers: 0

48 MPI ranks open the same twelve MERRA2 files. Not one met handle in the entire run is
the only reader of its object. A replay that models "one prefetcher per open, scored in
isolation" is therefore modelling something that does not occur in this workload, and all
three symptoms above are the same cause:

  (a) FIDELITY. The mount suppresses a dispatch for a block a sibling handle already has
      in flight or cached, so the recorded `dispatched` is smaller than the replayed
      Observe() output. This necessarily hits prefetching handles hardest — a handle can
      only have a dispatch suppressed if it tried to dispatch. Hence 135/143.
  (b) DENOMINATOR. 143 met handles each decide to prefetch most of a ~400 MB object:
      51.7 GiB of decisions against 3.94 GB the mount actually fetched, 13.4x. The
      guard's text says "too far apart to be dedup alone"; on this workload it IS dedup
      alone, at ~50x sharing.
  (c) COLD TAX. Per-handle net waste on HEMCO is 8431.0 / 8488.5 MiB, which EXCEEDS the
      mount's entire waste budget (s3_bytes 9.0183 - distinct 3.6979 = 5.32 GB from all
      causes combined). Bytes a sibling reads are counted as this handle's waste.

The gate-5h measurement was the first sight of this: 2.0-2.4x over on a synthetic arm with
6 handles sharing 1 object. At GCHP's real sharing it is 13.4x, and it stops being a
correction factor and becomes a category error.


4. WHAT THE CAPTURE DOES ANSWER — THE FLOOR'S MULTIPLIER
---------------------------------------------------------
P5 pre-registered the #256 floor at 1.0-1.4 GB (falsifier < 0.5 GB). The per-handle
instrument reports HEMCO net 8.43 GB, six times over — but section 3(c) shows that
quantity is inflated past the mount's total waste, so it does not score the prediction.
The prediction named a MOUNT-level quantity (`fill_bytes{demand}`), measured directly in
gates 5c/5f at ~1.23 GB and constant across eight policies. That remains the answer; the
capture does not refine it.

What the capture adds is the missing MULTIPLIER, which no previous gate could see:

     HEMCO first-run pre-decision cold reads:  24,762 (arm a) / 24,568 (arm b)
       over 6,272 handles = 3.9 per handle
       over   204 keys    = ~121 per object
     met first-run cold reads:                  5,884 / 6,160  over 12 keys

Gate 5f found the mechanism (a per-handle cold-start granularity tax at fs.go:778-786,
byte-exact only in `Random`, so a `cold` handle is fetched whole-chunk). Gate 5f could
only show it on ONE handle. The capture shows the mechanism is paid ~121 times per HEMCO
object, because 30-50 ranks each cold-start independently on the same file. That is the
floor's structure: not a few pathological handles, but every rank paying the same
entry fee on the same object.

It also closes gate 5b's open question from the other side. Gate 5b concluded the waste
"needs cross-handle state" because no per-handle flag could reach it (eight policies, one
constant floor). The capture shows why that had to be true: the detector's state is
per-open, the waste is per-open, and there are 30-50 opens per object. No setting of a
per-open window can see the other 49.


5. WHAT UPSTREAM SHOULD DO WITH THE SCORER
-------------------------------------------
The instrument behaved correctly and that is why this gate produced a result instead of a
false one. Its fidelity gate and both new implausibility guards fired on exactly the arms
that deserved them (met 8.86x flagged, HEMCO 0.94x not flagged), and without them the
"SEPARATION, rho = 0.616" line would have been reported as the campaign's answer.

To make the rule answerable, the replay has to model the SHARED CACHE, not a lone handle:
replay all handles of a mount against one simulated cache, suppress a dispatch for a block
already resident or in flight, and credit a dispatch as used if ANY handle subsequently
reads it. That is `byte_follow_through_global`, asked for in gate 5g as an interpretive
nicety; the capture shows it is the only unit in which the question has an answer on this
workload. The per-handle column remains meaningful as a strict lower bound.

Second, the minimum-n and tie-mass guards still matter: on the faithful subset the tool
would otherwise have printed a verdict from n = 3.


6. STATUS AND COST
-------------------
Cost: two jobs on one c8g.48xlarge, ~35 min of node time total (three ~390 s runs plus
mounts and one wasted mount cycle), well inside the single-job estimate.

Harness bug worth recording: job 21 lost arms a and b to `(( miss )) && { ...; return 1; }`
as the last statement of mount_lith — with miss=0 the arithmetic is false, so the FUNCTION
returned 1 and both traced arms were skipped after mounting cleanly and creating their
traces. The guard added to prevent a silent capture failure became one. Fixed with an
explicit `return 0`; arm c was unaffected because it skips that block, and it ran, which is
why the perturbation control exists at all. Resubmitted onto the still-warm node.

Also measured at $0 before the job, and reported to #264: with --daemon, an unwritable
--pf-trace path still mounts successfully AND the `level=ERROR "prefetch trace disabled"`
goes to /tmp/lith-<uid>-mount.log, i.e. the one failure mode that costs a full run and
yields nothing is both non-fatal and invisible in exactly the mode a capture uses. The
harness therefore proves the trace files exist before launching mpirun.
