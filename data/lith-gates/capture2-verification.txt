ACCEPTANCE CHECK, and a correction to how it was stated. Upstream asked for "`seq` strictly
increasing within each fh". Driving my own checker on the gate 5i traces BEFORE the job
showed that reading is not usable: the c64 trace — which replays at exactly 1.00x under
#273 — fails it at 0.36%, because rows are APPENDED outside w.mu by design. The scoreable
property is that seq is a TOTAL ORDER (unique, positive); the file-order inversion rate is
then a measurement of how much the sort matters, not a defect. Scored that way both mounts
pass, and the inversion rate (0.46-0.54%) is the direct evidence that #272/#273 is load
bearing on real GCHP traces.


2. THE VERDICT: SEPARATION — and it is not an artefact of the racy column it rests on
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pre-registered rule (#256, agreed in writing before either side had data): SEPARATION iff a
feature of a handle's first k<=8 reads predicts per-handle BYTE follow-through at Spearman
|rho| >= 0.5, fit on one arm and tested on another.

  feature                met/a    met/b    hemco/a   hemco/b
  mean_abs_gap_blocks    +0.635   +0.634   +0.271    +0.266    <- best
  max_abs_gap_blocks     +0.624   +0.627   +0.235    +0.254
  frac_large_gap         +0.221   +0.198   +0.061    +0.064
  mean_read_kib          +0.084   +0.110   -0.150    -0.122
  frac_monotonic         -0.017   +0.031   +0.039    +0.069
  frac_straddle          -0.034   -0.033   +0.023    -0.056
  AUC(met vs hemco) = 0.651 (n=283 vs 334)

VERDICT: SEPARATION on met, reproduced out-of-sample (0.635 fit / 0.634 test). NOT on HEMCO,
where the best feature reaches 0.27 — i.e. the mount whose waste the campaign is about is
the one where a handle's first reads DO NOT predict whether its prefetch is used.

THE OBVIOUS OBJECTION, tested at $0 rather than left as a caveat. The winning feature is
built from `gap`, and gate 5i showed `gap` is racy: computed from h.lastReadEnd.Load()
BEFORE the lock, stored AFTER it, so on a contended handle a read can be classified against
another read's endpoint. met is exactly the contended shape. So the feature was recomputed
from a REPAIRED gap (off minus the previous end of the same fh, in seq order):

  class    rho(RECORDED gap)   rho(REPAIRED gap)   rho(recorded, repaired)   racy first-8 gaps
  met/a         +0.635              +0.637               +0.994                    13
  met/b         +0.634              +0.637               +0.994                    20
  hemco/a       +0.271              +0.270               +1.000                   139
  hemco/b       +0.266              +0.266               +0.999                   123

The signal is carried by the honest part of the column. Repairing the race moves rho by
+0.002 and would not change any verdict. (This does not re-run the replay: feeding repaired
gaps into the state machine would trip the fidelity check by construction, because the mount
really did decide on the racy values. What is testable — and tested — is whether the
PREDICTIVE signal survives.)


3. P7 PARTIALLY FALSIFIED — the window is small, but it is not pinned at the floor
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gate 5i predicted, from inverting perHandleWindow = clamp(budgetBlocks/open_handles, 2,
--max-readahead): max_window = 2 on essentially every HEMCO row, 2-3 on met. Measured:

  mount   min   median   max    modes
  met      4      8       29    8x4231, 7x3418, 6x2840, 5x2840, 10x2764
  HEMCO    2      3      223    2x12704, 39x8314, 3x4684, 4x1895, 5x1055

So the DIRECTION holds and the magnitude claim does not. The window in force is 1-2 orders
of magnitude below the 223 ceiling for nearly all reads (median 3 on HEMCO, 8 on met), which
is the mechanism gate 5b needed: --max-readahead was not binding, so eight policy arms that
moved only the ceiling could not have moved the hit rate. But HEMCO is NOT uniformly at the
floor — there is a 39-block mode (8,314 rows) and a 223 tail — and met never reaches the
floor at all.

MY ESTIMATION ERROR, stated plainly: I predicted 2 and 2.38 by dividing budgetBlocks by the
handle count in the trace (6,272 and 598). Those are handles SEEN OVER THE RUN, not handles
OPEN AT ONCE. The real concurrency is much lower and varies, which is exactly why #271's
column had to exist rather than be derived. The lesson is the one the whole gate sequence
keeps re-teaching: a quantity reconstructed from a proxy is not the quantity.


4. THE RESIDUAL REPLAY DEFECT — sorting is necessary, not sufficient
--------------------------------------------------------------------
10 of 6229 handles (arm a) and 9 of 6248 (arm b) still diverge from the mount after #273's
sort. They are a specific, reproducible population, not noise:

                    rows(median/max)   obj_size(median)   parts rows   state_after seen
  MISMATCHING           12 / 33           33.4 MB          0 of 10     cold 89, random 67
  faithful, >=10 rows    -  / 510         107.8 MB         14 of 794   cold 15871, sequential
                                                                        4507, random 4131,
                                                                        strided 120

The signature is state, not size or the parts path: EVERY row of a mismatching handle is
`cold` or `random` and none is ever `sequential` or `strided`. Same in both arms (89/67 and
88/65). max_window variation does NOT separate them (5 of 10 vary, vs 317 of 794 faithful),
and the gap race does not (median 0 racy first-8 gaps). So the remaining divergence is in
the replay's reconstruction of the cold->random path on handles that never establish.
Keys are ship_plume_lut_*.txt and AEIC_monmean_*.nc. Filed upstream.


5. WHAT IS STILL NOT ANSWERED
------------------------------
P5 THE FLOOR: HEMCO cold NET waste 8403 MiB (a) / 8513 MiB (b) per-handle, against a
predicted 1.0-1.4 GB and a mount budget of 5.32 GB. Per-handle netting double-counts a
shared cache — this is the same overflow capture 1 produced (8431 MiB), now reproduced in
two independent arms, which makes it a property of the UNIT rather than of a run.
The scorer's own decomposition says so: replay decisions 52,256 chunks = 1.00x the mount's
own column, mount fetched 15,213 => 3.42x genuine sharing dedup. met is read by 596 handles
over ~12 keys with 0 sole readers.

=> The per-handle denominator is settled as wrong, twice, at the same multiplier. The next
   piece of work is the SHARED-CACHE replay, which is offline and free. This job supplied
   its input: four traces that are, for the first time, in decision order and carrying the
   window the mount actually applied.
