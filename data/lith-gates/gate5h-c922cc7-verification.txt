1. THE EOF CLAMP WORKS — 12.3x OVER BECOMES 1.18x
--------------------------------------------------
Gate 5g's defect C1: the scorer reported 1117 dispatched blocks / 9.37 GB where
the mount's own lith_prefetch_issued_total said 91 and s3_bytes said the object
had been fetched exactly once. ~94% of the denominator lay past EOF.

c922cc7, same class of workload, with -issued 182 (= 91+91+0+0):

  denominator sanity: 215.9 MiB of dispatch DECISIONS against 106.6 MiB of
                      distinct objects (2.02x; >1x is normal)
  vs live lith_prefetch_issued_total: replay 215 chunk-decisions,
                                      mount 182 chunks fetched

215 vs 182 = 1.18x, against 12.3x before. Units are consistent: 215.9 MiB of
decisions / 1 MiB chunkSize = 215 chunk-decisions, and issued_total counts
1 MiB chunks. The clamp is real and obj_size lands correctly — every row of
all4.handles.csv carries obj_size = 111810271, the true value.

  Plumbing note: -issued is a single scalar compared PER TRACE. Run met-a alone
  with its own -issued 91 and the same 215 is compared against 91 = 2.36x; run
  all four and 215 is compared against the 182 summed over four different
  mounts. For a one-mount capture with N trace files the line should compare
  the SUMMED replay against the one issued count, or take -issued per trace.
  Reported.


2. THE REMAINING 2.0-2.4x IS NOT A BUG — IT IS THE PER-HANDLE CEILING, MEASURED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
denominator sanity 2.02x (215.9 MiB of decisions for a 106.6 MiB object) and
vs-live 2.36x (215 decisions, 91 chunks fetched) are the SAME fact: multiple
handles decide to prefetch overlapping blocks, the global cache fetches each
once, and a per-handle replay cannot dedupe them.

So gate 5g's caveat C7 is now quantified rather than asserted: per-handle byte
follow-through is a LOWER BOUND on prefetch's value, and on this arm the gap
is 2.0-2.4x. Under 48 ranks sharing met and HEMCO files the factor will be
larger. byte_follow_through_global alongside byte_follow_through_handle is
therefore load-bearing for interpreting the capture, not cosmetic — and
-issued is the instrument that makes the gap visible.


3. NET COLD TAX SEPARATES THE TWO MOUNTS — AND NET == GROSS ON HEMCO
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Gate 5g's defect C5: waste = chunkSize - read_len never netted out coverage by
the same handle's later reads, so a contiguous reader's waste was largely
fictional. c922cc7 reports both:

  class   NET waste     gross      genuine fraction
  met      5.0 MiB      21.0 MiB     24%
  hemco   95.6 MiB      95.6 MiB    100%

Per handle (all4.handles.csv):
  met/a fh1   (241 rows, pure stream)  net        0  of gross 8,257,536  ->   0%
  met/a fh2-6 (16 rows each)           net  1,048,576 of gross 2,752,512 ->  38%
  hemco/*     (48 rows each)           net 16,711,680 = gross 16,711,680 -> 100%
                                       twelve handles for twelve, exactly

Two consequences:

  (a) The #256 floor is confirmed as genuine waste, not an accounting artifact.
      On HEMCO-shaped handles the netting changes nothing, so the pre-registered
      1.0-1.4 GB prediction (falsifier < 0.5 GB) will be scored on the quantity
      it named. This was the open worry in the #256 comment; it is closed.

  (b) 16,711,680 B is reproduced exactly, twelve for twelve, for pure-64 KiB
      scatter handles. Gate 5f's b2 handle reported 16,646,144 only because the
      kernel merged its FIRST read to 128 KiB. Both numbers are right for their
      handle; the mechanism's arithmetic is unchanged.

The netting also does real interpretive work: it takes a single number that
looked like waste on both mounts and shows it is ~0% genuine on the streaming
reader and 100% genuine on the scattered one. That is the per-handle version of
gate 5f's met-1.8%-vs-HEMCO-33% residual split.


4. CLASS/ARM PAIRING FIXED — LABEL ORDER NO LONGER DECIDES
-----------------------------------------------------------
Gate 5g's defect C2: AUC used byLabel[labels[0]] vs byLabel[labels[1]], so the
natural ordering of four traces compared a class against ITSELF (measured 0.500,
which the rule reads as evidence of no separation).

c922cc7 groups by the class prefix of `class/arm=file`. Passing
  met/a= met/b= hemco/a= hemco/b=
-- the exact order that used to make AUC compare met against met -- now yields
output identical to any other ordering. The artifact cannot be decided by
argument order any more.

And defect C3, the silent drop when one class dispatches nothing, is fixed
loudly:

  ** class "hemco" has NO handles that dispatched prefetch: the AUC half of the
     rule cannot be evaluated for it, and "no separation" must NOT be concluded
     from its absence. **

That guard fires on exactly the population it was written for: hemco's twelve
handles are all NaN follow-through, 0 blocks dispatched, because 7 MiB gaps keep
the detector in Random.


5. STILL OPEN: SEPARATION IS DECLARED ON ONE EFFECTIVE DEGREE OF FREEDOM
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The minimum-n and tie-mass guards asked for in gate 5g do not appear in
c922cc7. The verdict block still prints:

  frac_large_gap   met/a=-1.000  met/b=-1.000  hemco=(no prefetching handles)
                   <- |rho|>=0.5 on 2+ arms (survives out-of-sample)
  best |rho| = 1.000 (frac_large_gap); threshold 0.5
  VERDICT: SEPARATION — a feature of a handle's first reads predicts byte
           follow-through.

The data behind rho = -1.000, from all4.handles.csv, is met/a's three non-NaN
handles:

  fh   byte_follow_through   frac_large_gap
   1        0.155852             0.000
   2        0.000000             0.125
   6        0.000000             0.125

n = 3, a two-way tie on BOTH axes, one handle differing: perfectly monotone by
construction, one effective degree of freedom. Worse, the winning feature is
confounded with eligibility — fh1 is the only handle that read enough rows
(241 vs 16) to be scored at all, and frac_large_gap = 0 is a restatement of
that. The AUC half cannot run (correctly, and loudly), so the verdict rests
entirely on this.

The out-of-sample annotation is now correct per the rule (met/a and met/b are
different arms of the same class, which is the agreed train/test structure),
but replicating a degenerate fit on a replicate of the same synthetic workload
does not add a degree of freedom. On the real capture n will be large and this
matters less — but a degenerate real subset (few HEMCO handles ever dispatching,
which is precisely the mechanism these arms exhibit) would trip it, and the
guard is cheap.


6. THE `size` COLUMN'S VALUE, REPRODUCED INDEPENDENTLY — AND IT IS WORSE THAN
   UPSTREAM MEASURED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Upstream reported that a main-binary (size-less) capture would score 1.000
where the truth is 0.407. Tested by stripping the `size` column out of the
c922cc7 traces and rescoring the identical reads:

  met WITH size:     3 prefetching handles  follow-through median 0.000 mean 0.052
                     denominator 215.9 MiB of decisions vs 106.6 MiB distinct (2.02x)
  met WITHOUT size:  1 prefetching handle   follow-through median 1.000 mean 1.000
                     denominator  14.1 MiB of decisions vs 210.8 MiB distinct (0.07x)
                     ** no `size` column: object sizes ESTIMATED from each
                        handle's highest read offset.

Confirmed, and in the same direction, larger: mean 0.052 -> 1.000. Two findings
upstream did not state:

  (a) The POPULATION is biased, not only the ratio. The prefetching-handle count
      drops 3 -> 1: two handles that genuinely dispatched are estimated to have
      such a small object that all their dispatches clamp away, and they leave
      the denominator entirely. A size-less capture doesn't just inflate the
      score, it silently discards the handles whose prefetch was most wasteful.

  (b) The sanity line itself goes wrong in BOTH directions and reads as
      reassuring. Without `size`, per-handle size estimates cannot be deduped
      onto one object, so "distinct objects" INFLATES while decisions COLLAPSE:
      met 106.6 -> 210.8 MiB, hemco 106.6 -> 633.0 MiB (= 6 x 105.5, one per
      handle). The 2.02x becomes 0.07x — which reads as "denominator is fine,
      plenty of headroom" at exactly the moment it is broken. The ESTIMATED
      warning does print, so the net exists; the ratio should be suppressed or
      marked rather than printed as a number.

hemco WITHOUT size: net waste 92.6 MiB vs 95.6 MiB with — the floor number is
only ~3% sensitive to the column, because a scatter handle's dispatch total is
zero either way. The inflation is a streaming-handle effect.


7. WHAT THIS DECIDES ABOUT THE CAPTURE
---------------------------------------
Buy it. The three defects that would have voided its verdict (denominator,
arm pairing, silent zero-prefetch class) are fixed and verified on live mounts;
the cold-tax quantity my pre-registered 1.0-1.4 GB is scored against is now the
net one, and net == gross on HEMCO-shaped handles.

Carry into the capture:
  - build lith from c922cc7 on the compute node, not main: a size-less trace
    would report ~1.000 follow-through and drop the wasteful handles
  - pass -issued <lith_prefetch_issued_total> and read the denominator-sanity
    and vs-live lines FIRST
  - expect per-handle follow-through to be a lower bound by a factor >= 2 under
    48 ranks, and report the cross-handle gap rather than the per-handle number
    alone
  - HEMCO on the real mount DOES dispatch (gate 5 measured prefetch_issued
    4750), so its AUC half should be evaluable; these surrogates are more
    extreme than reality and that limits what arms 4-5 say about GCHP


PROBE CAVEATS
-------------
These are instrument tests on real S3 objects, not evidence about GCHP's
handles. The met surrogate's 6 handles read overlapping spans of ONE object, so
the page cache absorbed most reads of handles 2-6 (16 rows recorded each vs
fh1's 241) — which is why n = 3 rather than 6 and why section 5's degeneracy is
so sharp. Every defect and fix above is a property of the tool and reproducible
from the logs.

Cosmetic bug in my own script: line 78's heading used backticks inside a
double-quoted echo, so `size` ran as a command ("size: 'a.out': No such file"
in the log). Affected the heading text only.
