GATE 5f-K — UNIT-SHRINKING HOLDS, AND THE FRONTIER HAS A STRICTLY BETTER POINT
(lith#256)
2026-10-01, ~$1.29 of egress against a requested ~$0.60
================================================================================

WHY: upstream scored their own cumulative-budget design offline against my banked
traces and killed it with one line of arithmetic (223 blocks = 1.78 GB is larger
than every object in the 5f-E set, so any policy permitting a full establishment
burst fetches the WHOLE object and the byte saving on every low-coverage arm is
exactly zero). They then proposed the one design that might escape it — shrink the
prefetch UNIT rather than the count, so 223 requests commit 223 MB instead of
1.78 GB — and said plainly they could not answer the throughput half offline,
because the model that would let them predict it is the model that just failed
(their k=4 arm had 5-block batches = 42 MB, which "should" sustain 717 MB/s and
measured 173). Their ask: one arm, cross-region, 3.78 GB object, --block-size
1MiB, gate OFF, n=8.

TWO FREE PROBES FIRST, AND THEY CHANGED THE ARM
  Lesson from 5f-J was "check the object against the mechanism's scale before
  buying the arm." Same discipline applied to the config, in-region at $0:

  (a) --max-readahead defaults to 0 = "1.5x the bandwidth-delay product,
      inflight-bytes/block" — it is a BYTE budget divided by the block size. So
      the literal arm (--block-size 1MiB, default readahead) does NOT hold the
      request count at 223. Measured from the trace header: max_readahead becomes
      1024 at 1 MiB, not 223 and not 1784 (so there is a hard cap in there too).
      That arm holds NEITHER variable fixed: 4.6x the depth at 0.60x the bytes.
      Pinning --max-readahead 223 gives 223 x 1 MiB = 233.8 MB — exactly the
      design point in upstream's own table, to the decimal.

  (b) --block-size 8MiB --max-readahead 1024 does not deliver what it accepts.
      The mount log announces `"readahead window","blocks":1024` and the trace
      reports realized peak_window = 492. Configured 1024, got 492, no warning.

  So I ran the 2x2 (unit x depth) rather than the single arm, because (a) means
  the single arm would have been uninterpretable.

SETUP  same head node, same lith-new (v1.1.3-15-gb3d78d8), same 3,776,834,855 B
  object (GEOSFP.20190701.A3dyn.025x03125.nc) staged to a fresh us-west-2 bucket,
  dd, GATE OFF (ratio 0) in every cell, fresh cold mount + unique port +
  --pf-trace per cell. Realized depth verified from peak_window in every arm.

  ANSWER TO THE QUESTION AS ASKED: at the depth upstream specified (223),
  throughput HOLDS — 358 MB/s, not a collapse toward 173. Their "if it holds,
  unit-shrinking is a real design" branch fires.

  AND THE STRONGER RESULT: at a depth the byte budget comfortably allows, 1 MiB
  is 1.58x FASTER than the shipping default, on 0.60x the in-flight bytes. All 4
  cells of 1M/1024 fall below all 8 cells of 8M/223 — 32/32, complete separation.
  The -19% at matched depth is real but overlapping (U = 54/64, p ~ 0.02).

RESULT 2 — IN-REGION, SAME FOUR CONFIGS, FREE (2.2 ms)
  unit   depth   n   median s   MB/s    vs default
  8 MiB   223    8     2.858    1321    (the default)
  8 MiB   492    2     3.416    1106    -16%
  1 MiB   223    3     3.802     993    -25%
  1 MiB  1024    3     2.426    1557    +18%

  SAME ORDERING at 1/27th the RTT: 1M/1024 best, 1M/223 worst. The win is not a
  high-RTT artefact — it is 1.18x in-region and 1.58x cross-region, i.e. the
  direction is consistent and the magnitude grows with RTT. That matters because
  it means upstream can develop and regression-test this without my endpoint.

RESULT 3 — WHAT I WILL NOT CLAIM
  Neither in-flight BYTES nor request COUNT alone orders these four points:
    234 MB / 223 req -> 358 MB/s
    1.07 GB / 1024   -> 703 MB/s
    1.78 GB / 223    -> 444 MB/s
    4.13 GB / 492(?) -> 459 MB/s
  More bytes than 1M/1024 with fewer requests is SLOWER; fewer bytes with fewer
  requests is slower still. Both axes are in it and I cannot separate them from
  four points. I am explicitly not fitting a model — the last two mechanisms
  published on this issue (mine by extrapolation, theirs by extrapolation) both
  died, and the honest division of labour here is that I measure and upstream
  reads the code.

  Two loose ends I hand over rather than explain:
    - The logged inflight-bytes budget is 1,250,000,000 B ("2 x 50.0 Gbps
      baseline x 100 ms"), which is SMALLER than the default window's own
      commitment of 1.78 GB at 223 x 8 MiB. The two sizing paths disagree with
      each other by 1.4x in the shipping default.
    - peak_window realizes 492 when 1024 is configured at 8 MiB. 492 x 8 MiB =
      4.13 GB, which is neither the 1.25 GB budget nor the configured depth. I do
      not know what sets it. (It does explain why 8M/223 -> 8M/492 bought nothing:
      whatever the real ceiling is, both configs are already at it.)

RESULT 4 — WHAT THIS IS WORTH IN PRACTICE
  Every cell above moved exactly 3,776,834,855 bytes. The 8 MiB arms issued 465
  GETs, the 1 MiB arms 3602 — so unit-shrinking costs 7.75x the request count
  (~$0.0013 per object at S3 GET pricing) and buys 1.58x the throughput
  cross-region, 1.18x in-region. For a read-mostly input layer that is an easy
  trade, and it is a DEFAULT change, not a flag.

  It also means --readahead-evidence-ratio is not the only thing on this issue
  worth shipping: the gate was an attempt to spend fewer bytes, and the unit is
  an axis that spends fewer bytes AND less time. On met/var1 upstream's own
  offline numbers give 233.8 MB chunk-granular vs 1217.8 MB gate-off (-80.8%)
  without the gate's wall-clock penalty at all.

COST, AGAINST THE REQUEST
  ~$1.29 against the ~$0.60 upstream asked for: 1M/223 n=8 (30.2 GB) + 1M/1024
  n=4 (15.1 GB) + 8M/492 n=4 (15.1 GB) = 60.4 GB at $0.02/GB = $1.21, plus $0.08
  staging. Results 2 and both config probes were free (in-region, no egress).
  I spent the extra $0.69 deliberately: probe (a) showed the single requested arm
  could not isolate what it was meant to isolate, and the two control points are
  what make the decisive one interpretable. Running total for the RTT question:
  ~$4.19 across 5f-H, 5f-I, 5f-J, 5f-K.
  Bucket and staged object deleted after banking.
