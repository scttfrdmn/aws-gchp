THE #256 RULE AT THE KEY LEVEL — pre-registration
==================================================
2026-09-20. $0: offline, on capture 2's four banked traces. No cluster run.
Written and committed BEFORE the scorer existed. Results append below, under RESULTS.

WHY THIS CUT

  The shared-cache replay found that 84-85% of all redeemed prefetch bytes are read by a
  DIFFERENT handle than the one that fetched them. If follow-through is not a property of
  the handle, then a predictor of it — if one exists — is a property of the OBJECT's access
  pattern across all its readers. Upstream (lith#256) accepted the shared unit as #274 and
  asked for exactly this cut, plus one thing more:

    "the same features scored against the COLD-START waste rather than follow-through. If a
     key-level feature predicts which objects pay the ~121x entry fee, that is directly the
     eligibility signal an object-scoped hint would key on."

  So there are two targets, and the second is the one that matters: the floor is the
  confirmed half (P5, ~1.23 GB, now agreed by live counters across twelve policies and by
  the offline replay to within 8%), and an object-scoped granularity hint is the only fix
  direction still standing after twelve fetch-policy flags failed to move it.

THE UNIT AND THE ROW

  One row per (label, arm, key). 204 keys on HEMCO, 12 on met. Targets and cold waste come
  from globalScore's shared-cache accounting, attributed per key instead of per handle — the
  same dedup, the same EOF clamp, the same first-run-cold rule, no second definition.

  FULL features use all of a key's reads. EARLY features use only the key's first k=8 reads
  in mount-wide `seq` order. The distinction is the whole point of actionability: a hint has
  to be decided from what is known when the object is first touched, not from the run's
  history. A FULL-feature correlation is description; an EARLY-feature correlation is a
  candidate policy.

TARGETS

  T1  follow_through          usedBytes / dispatchedBytes for the key (shared unit)
  T2  cold_waste_bytes        the key's deduped first-run cold net waste (raw bytes)
  T3  cold_waste_per_distinct T2 / distinct bytes read on the key — the key-level analogue
                              of the mount-level headline (HEMCO 33% vs met 1.8%)

  T2 is scale-dependent by construction (a bigger object can waste more), so T3 is the
  binding target. Registered now so T2's rho cannot be quoted as the finding later.

FEATURES (both FULL and EARLY/first-8)

  readers, reads, reads_per_reader, mean_read_kib, frac_small (len <= byte-exact threshold
  AND < chunk, i.e. the cold tax's own eligibility test), distinct_frac (union bytes read /
  object size), span_frac, overlap_frac (1 - union / sum of per-reader distinct: 0 = readers
  partition the object, ->1 = they replicate each other), frac_monotonic_mount,
  mean_abs_gap_blocks_mount, interleave (fraction of consecutive reads on the key that come
  from a different handle), frac_straddle.

  overlap_frac and interleave are the two that cannot exist at the handle level. They are
  the reason to do this at all.

PREDICTIONS, with falsifiers

  K1  T1: distinct_frac is the best FULL predictor, rho >= +0.5 on both HEMCO arms.
      Falsifier: |rho| < 0.3.
  K2  T2: readers gives |rho| >= 0.5 — expected and near-mechanical (more readers, more cold
      entries). Declared in advance as NOT a finding; it is a sanity check on the plumbing.
  K3  T3: frac_small is the best predictor, rho >= +0.5 on both HEMCO arms.
      Falsifier: |rho| < 0.3, which would mean there is no key-level eligibility signal on
      the target that matters — a negative result, and still worth posting.
  K4  actionability: the best EARLY |rho| on T3 is >= 0.7x the best FULL |rho| on T3.
      Falsifier: < 0.5x, which says the first 8 reads of an object do not reveal whether it
      will pay the tax, and an object-scoped hint would need a static rule (path family,
      size class) rather than an online one.
  K5  concentration: the top 10% of HEMCO keys hold >= 50% of HEMCO's cold waste.
      Falsifier: < 30%. This one matters independently of every rho: if the floor is
      concentrated, a mediocre predictor plus a small hint budget still recovers most of it.
  K6  recovery-at-budget: ranking keys by the best EARLY feature and hinting the top 10% of
      keys recovers >= 50% of what the ORACLE ranking (rank by actual waste) recovers at the
      same budget. Falsifier: < 25%. This is K3/K4 restated in the units of the fix, and it
      is the number an implementer would actually want.
  K7  met: n=12 keys. Below any usable n on both minDistinct and --min-n. Met's rho values
      are reported as a control but MUST NOT be counted toward a verdict, and met's failure
      to fit is not evidence of anything. Registered so it cannot be cherry-picked either
      way after the fact.

  Cross-arm rule, as upstream's: a feature counts only if both arms agree in sign and both
  clear the threshold, and upstream's own `degenerate` gate (>= minDistinct distinct values
  on both axes) applies unchanged.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SETTLE, KNOWN IN ADVANCE

  - No eviction, inherited from the shared-cache pass: an upper bound on redemption, tight
    on these traces (3.0/3.4 GiB distinct vs 24/32 GB cache) and unsafe in general.
  - A key-level correlation is not a mechanism. Even K3+K4+K6 all passing would license a
    HINT experiment, not a claim about what lith should do by default.
  - The traces are one workload (GCHP 14.7.1 C24 fullchem, 48 ranks) on two mounts. The
    arms are re-runs of the same workload, so cross-arm agreement tests reproducibility,
    NOT generalisation to other readers.


RESULTS
=======

BUILT

  cmd/lith-pfreplay/keys.go (patches/lith/pfreplay-keys.go) + 17 additive edits via
  patches/lith/pfreplay-keys-wire.py, on upstream's PR #274 tree (b3d78d8, which is the
  shared-cache patch as they merged it). `go vet`, `gofmt` and upstream's own
  replay_test.go all clean. Behind -keys, which implies -global because the accounting is
  the shared one. globalScore gained a fourth return value — the per-key aggregates — so
  the key-level numbers come out of the same loops as the mount-level ones and the dedup,
  the EOF clamp and the first-run-cold rule cannot drift between units.

  Plumbing check first, because the last three gates each found a defect in an instrument
  before finding anything about lith: summed over keys, the pass reproduces the mount
  totals exactly — HEMCO 4822.8 MiB dispatched, 607.4 used, 0.126 follow-through, 1257.3
  MiB cold net waste; met 3185.3 / 2362.6 / 0.742 / 48.7. Same numbers as the per-handle
  roll-up, one aggregation apart.

0. FIRST, A CORRECTION TO UPSTREAM'S #274 NOTE

  Upstream reported the cold waste as "1,257.3 and 1,259.4 MiB here, closer to the live
  1,224-1,239 MB than your 1,318/1,321 — the difference is #274's conditional-Open() fix."

  1257.3 MiB IS 1318.4 MB. 1259.4 MiB is 1320.6 MB. They are the same two numbers in
  different units, and this replay run on upstream's own #274 tree reproduces them
  bit-identically (data/lith-gates/key-level-fit.log, and the head-to-head in
  replay-274.log). The live figures are decimal MB: the twelve-policy table's first column
  is lith_s3_bytes_total/1e6 (8.891096602e+09 -> 8891.1), so the honest comparison is
  1318.4 MB replay vs 1224.3 MB measured = 7.7% high, which is what was banked.

  So #274's conditional-Open() fix moved the cold-waste estimate by ZERO. What it did fix
  is fidelity: HEMCO's 10/6229 and 9/6248 diverging handles are GONE on the #274 tree — all
  four traces now replay with zero mismatches, and the key-level pass reports 0 objects
  with a diverged reader. That is a real improvement and it is the one worth crediting.

1. THE PRE-REGISTERED SCORECARD

  K1  T1 / distinct_frac >= +0.5        FAILED as stated. +0.449 / +0.419 on HEMCO: above
      the 0.3 falsifier, below the bar. The qualifying feature on T1 is reads_per_reader
      (+0.615 / +0.627) — but see section 3: it is partly mechanical and its EARLY version
      is +0.098 / +0.166, i.e. nothing.
  K2  T2 / readers >= 0.5               PASSED, +0.639 / +0.607, and as declared in advance
      this is plumbing, not a finding.
  K3  T3 / frac_small >= +0.5           FAILED, and the reason is the finding. frac_small is
      a CONSTANT 1.000 on all 204 HEMCO objects AND all 12 met objects: every read on both
      mounts is sub-chunk. It cannot predict anything within a class because it does not
      vary. The named guess was wrong; the target does have a qualifying predictor —
      distinct_frac at -0.689 / -0.707 — see section 2.
  K4  EARLY/FULL >= 0.7 on T3           FAILED at 0.59 (HEMCO-only: 0.417/0.707), above the
      0.5 falsifier. The first eight reads carry about 60% of the signal's strength.
  K5  top 10% hold >= 50% of the floor  FALSIFIED. 21.8% / 21.9%, below the 30% falsifier.
      58 of 204 objects hold half of HEMCO's floor. The floor is DIFFUSE.
  K6  EARLY ranking >= 0.5 of oracle    PASSED. distinct_frac recovers 0.72-0.76 of the
      oracle at a 10% budget, 0.83-0.90 at 25%. Heavily qualified in section 4.
  K7  met may not vote                  Honoured. Met's rho reaches 0.949 on T3 and is
      excluded from every verdict above; n=12 objects.

  Two of seven passed as stated. Every pre-registered FEATURE guess was wrong; the
  pre-registered METHOD found the answer anyway, which is the argument for writing the
  falsifiers down rather than the predictions.

2. THE ANSWER TO UPSTREAM'S QUESTION: COVERAGE, NOT CONCURRENCY

  On the binding target — cold waste per byte actually read — the predictor is how much of
  the object the workload reads:

    feature (FULL)         hemco/a   hemco/b    counts?
    distinct_frac           -0.689    -0.707    YES, both arms, same sign
    interleave              -0.328    -0.328    no
    readers                 +0.349    +0.288    no
    reads_per_reader        +0.088    +0.107    no
    mean_abs_gap_blocks     +0.295    +0.295    no

  distinct_frac shares a term with the target (T3 = W/D, feature = D/S), so the sign could
  in principle be induced. Permutation null, 2000 shuffles of W per arm with D and S left
  attached to their objects (scripts/lith/keylevel-artifact-null.py):

    null mean +0.172, 95% [+0.036, +0.303], p(null <= observed) < 0.0005 on both arms.

  The artifact runs the OTHER WAY, and the observed -0.689 is achieved against it. The
  artifact-free version of the same claim, rho(distinct_frac, raw W), is -0.469 / -0.476:
  just under the bar, which is why the null test is what licenses quoting the normalised
  figure.

  MECHANISM CORRECTED 2026-09-26 (upstream caught it; verified three ways, see section 7).
  I wrote that the null is positive "because rho(D, W) = +0.55". That cannot be the
  reason: the permutation destroys which W belongs to which (D, S), so no statistic of
  that pairing can enter the null's expectation. The real driver is the D-S structure,
  -rho(distinct_frac, D) = +0.287 / +0.280. The conclusion is unchanged — the null was
  always computed empirically, never from rho(D,W) — and the p-value stands.

  So: the objects that pay the entry fee are the ones whose readers touch them SPARSELY.
  Not the ones with many readers (+0.35), not the ones read out of order (+0.29), not the
  ones whose readers interleave (-0.33).

3. THE CORRECTION THIS FORCES ON THE CAMPAIGN'S OWN STORY

  Per-class medians of the FULL features:

    feature              met      HEMCO
    frac_small          1.000    1.000     <- identical, and constant on every object
    mean_read_kib     122.014  103.814     <- essentially the same read size
    span_frac           1.000    1.000     <- both read end-to-end in SPAN
    distinct_frac       0.647    0.161     <- the difference
    readers            52.000   33.000
    interleave          0.494    0.787

  HEMCO is not "the mount that does small scattered reads". Met's reads are the same size
  and just as sub-chunk, and both mounts touch their objects from beginning to end. HEMCO
  is the mount with LOW COVERAGE: it reads 16% of each object where met reads 65%. Met's
  many small reads eventually pay for the chunks they fetch; HEMCO's never do. Every
  earlier description of this contrast in read-size terms was reading the wrong column.

  That also explains the sequential detector's behaviour without any appeal to
  interleaving: HEMCO's access is monotone (frac_monotonic 0.741) across the object's full
  span, so "sequential" is the correct classification and prefetch fires — it is just that
  93% of what gets fetched is never read, because the reader is walking a sparse hyperslab.

  And the byte-level mechanism, verified independently of keys.go against the raw traces:
  overlap_frac is EXACTLY 0 on all 216 objects of both mounts — the readers partition each
  object byte for byte, zero redundant reading anywhere — while 47.2% of HEMCO's touched
  1 MiB chunks are touched by more than one handle (met: 20.0%). The waste is not
  duplicated reads. It is granularity, and nothing else.

4. WHAT AN IMPLEMENTER GETS, WHICH IS LESS THAN THE RHO SUGGESTS

  Recovery at budget: rank objects, hint the top N%, count the waste those objects
  actually carry. Oracle ranks by the waste itself.

    budget   oracle   EARLY distinct_frac   obj_size (post-hoc)
    top  5%   12.5%    7.6-8.1% (0.60-0.64)   9.8-10.1% (0.78-0.81)
    top 10%   21.8%   15.6-16.5% (0.72-0.76)  19.5-19.7% (0.90)
    top 25%   45.1%   37.4-40.5% (0.83-0.90)  37.9-38.0% (0.84)

  obj_size is the most actionable candidate of all — the mount knows it at open(), before a
  single read — and it recovers 0.90 of the oracle at a 10% budget. But the oracle itself is
  weak, and worse:

    the top 10% LARGEST HEMCO objects hold 18.7% of the waste and 31.9% of the bytes.

  Size-ranking recovers LESS than its own byte share. It looks good only against a ceiling
  that is itself low. The honest reading of the whole table is K5: the floor is diffuse and
  roughly proportional to object size, so no small object list reaches it.

  Stated as implementable rules, on HEMCO:

    rule                objects hinted   share of the floor caught
    coverage < 0.25      142 / 204 (70%)        96.1%
    coverage < 0.50      151 / 204 (74%)        99.6%
    size > 32 MiB        145 / 204 (71%)        97.9%
    size > 8 MiB         165 / 204 (81%)       100.0%

  Any rule that catches the floor fires on ~70-80% of the working set. And it is not
  self-limiting to the pathological mount: coverage < 0.25 fires on 6 of met's 12 objects
  too (94.7% of met's much smaller floor).

  CONCLUSION, and it is the same one from the other direction: an object-scoped ALLOW-LIST
  is not the fix. A predictor of the tax exists at the key level, it reproduces, it
  survives a null, and it still does not license a small hint set — because what it
  predicts is a property of essentially the whole HEMCO working set. The fix direction gate
  5f named stands unchanged and unnarrowed: make the granularity commitment conditional on
  evidence ("unknown until proven sequential") rather than attaching hints to objects.

5. WHAT DOES CHANGE FOR #256

  The rule was being fit at the wrong level. At the handle level in the shared unit the
  best feature was frac_monotonic at +0.441 (PARTIAL, no qualifying feature). At the key
  level, on the same traces, the same code, and the same fidelity filter, two features
  qualify on two arms each: reads_per_reader (+0.615/+0.627) on follow-through, and
  distinct_frac (-0.689/-0.707) on the cold tax.

  With the caveat that matters for T1: reads_per_reader is partly definitional — an object
  nobody reads again cannot redeem a fetch — and its EARLY version is +0.098/+0.166. So on
  follow-through there is still no ACTIONABLE predictor, which is the same answer as
  before, now with a reason: the payoff is not predictable from anything visible when the
  decision is made. On the cold tax there is one, at 0.59 of full strength from the first
  eight reads.

6. STILL NOT SETTLED

  - No eviction, inherited. Upper bound on redemption; tight here, unsafe in general.
  - chunk_overlap, readers_per_chunk and obj_size are POST-HOC: invented after seeing the
    first run's output and marked with * in the tool so they cannot qualify a feature or set
    the best |rho| the verdict is read off. They are hypotheses for another workload.
  - One workload, two mounts, two arms of the same run. Cross-arm agreement is
    reproducibility, not generalisation.
  - A latent bug fixed in passing, which changed no number here: the first draft filtered
    FULL and EARLY fits with a shared NaN mask, which would have silently changed the FULL
    population to match EARLY's. It bit nothing on these traces because the degenerate
    EARLY features are constant rather than NaN — but it is the population-vs-unit confound
    from the shared-cache flip, one level down, and it would have bitten eventually.

7. WHY THE NULL IS POSITIVE — MY EXPLANATION WAS WRONG (2026-09-26, $0)

  Upstream reproduced every number, reimplemented the null from the stated method rather
  than running my script (their words: "reproducing a number by running the code that
  produced it is not a check") and got +0.171/+0.173, 95% [+0.037,+0.303] against my
  +0.172/[+0.036,+0.303]. Then they corrected the MECHANISM I had attached to it.

  THE ARGUMENT, which is correct: the null's distribution depends only on the multiset of
  W and the paired (D, S). The permutation destroys which object each W belonged to, so
  rho(D, W) — a property of the original pairing — CANNOT enter the null's expectation.

  T1, FORCING rho(D,W) TO ITS EXTREMES (re-attach the same W multiset to D monotonically):

    hemco/a   as measured   rho(D,W) = +0.551   null centre +0.1732
              forced        rho(D,W) = +0.993   null centre +0.1730
              forced        rho(D,W) = -0.993   null centre +0.1716

  Driving rho(D,W) across its entire range moves the null centre by 0.0016. My stated
  mechanism is dead; theirs survives.

  T2, DOES -rho(D/S, D) PREDICT THE NULL CENTRE? Not just on HEMCO — on all four arms,
  including the sign flip on met, which is the part that makes it a prediction:

    arm        n    rho(D,W)   rho(D/S,D)   -rho(D/S,D)   null centre   observed rho
    hemco/a  204     +0.551      -0.287        +0.287        +0.1732       -0.689
    hemco/b  204     +0.546      -0.280        +0.280        +0.1722       -0.707
    met/a     12     -0.854      +0.804        -0.804        -0.2673       -0.920
    met/b     12     -0.854      +0.812        -0.812        -0.2726       -0.913

  T3, THE TEST NEITHER OF US RAN: hold the W multiset fixed and DIAL the D-S structure by
  construction. If the mechanism is real the null centre must track it across the range:

    built so that          rho(D/S,D)   -rho(D/S,D)   null centre
    D/S independent of D     -0.110        +0.110        +0.0609
    D/S rises with D         +1.000        -1.000        -0.5544
    D/S falls with D         -1.000        +1.000        +0.5483

  It tracks, sign and rough magnitude, across the full range. That promotes their
  explanation from a plausible post-hoc mechanism to a tested one.

  WHY I GOT IT WRONG, AND WHY THE DATA DIDN'T CATCH ME: across the two mounts rho(D,W)
  and rho(D/S,D) happen to be ANTI-CORRELATED (hemco +0.55/-0.287, met -0.854/+0.804), so
  both explanations predict the same null SIGN on both mounts. The banked output contained
  met's negative null all along and it looked like confirmation. Only T1 — an intervention
  on the data, not another correlation in it — breaks the tie.

  AND A CAVEAT I OWE, found by finally reading met's line: on met the null runs TOWARD the
  observed sign (-0.27 against an observed -0.92), so met's coverage correlation is partly
  induced by the shared denominator, where HEMCO's is achieved against it. Pre-registered
  K7 barred met from voting for n=12; it turns out there was a second, independent reason.
  Nothing quoted in this report changes, because nothing quoted rests on met's rho.

  What does NOT change: the null was always computed by permutation, never derived from
  rho(D,W), so p < 0.0005 and the -0.689/-0.707 finding stand exactly as published. What
  changes is one sentence of explanation — and it slightly strengthens the result, since
  the bias is a real property of the workload's size/coverage structure (sparsely-read
  objects are systematically larger) rather than an accident of the waste distribution.
