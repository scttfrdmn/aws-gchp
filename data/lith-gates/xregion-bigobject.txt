RESULT 3 — THE TRACE LOCALIZES IT TO THE FIRST 12% OF THE READ
  cell            rows    nonzero win   min  max  first win=223 at   sum dispatched
  r0 (rep 5)      28815   28751         223  223  off 8.4 MB         672
  r4 (rep 5)      28815   28751           4  223  off 469.8 MB       672
  r4 (rep 2)      28815   28751           4  223  off 469.8 MB       672

  Identical row count, identical total dispatch (672 blocks), identical window
  trajectory between reps. 3520 of 28751 reads (12.24%) run with window < 223,
  and they are exactly the first 469.8/3776.8 = 12.44% of the object by offset.
  Crossover is where the formula puts it: 223 blocks x 8 MiB / 4 = 467.6 MB.

  Note for the record: dd bs=1M is irrelevant at this layer. FUSE delivers
  28,815 reads of 128 KiB = the object. That is WHY dd, dd128 and cat were all
  +0.0% in 5f-G — they were never different readers below the kernel.

  So a cap that binds over 12% of the read costs 160% of the wall. Either the
  penalty is enormous but local, or something set while capped persists after.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION, WRITTEN AND COMMITTED BEFORE THE NEXT BYTE MOVED
  The crossover offset is 223 x 8 MiB / R, so the ratio knob is a direct lever on
  the length of the capped prefix:
      R = 1  -> 1870 MB (49.5% of the object)
      R = 4  ->  468 MB (12.4%)   [measured above]
      R = 40 ->   47 MB (1.24%)

  H_prefix: the excess is paid only while capped, so excess scales with prefix
    length. First-order, from the measured 13.23 s at R=4:
      R=40 -> excess ~1.3 s, wall ~9.8 s   (near ratio-0's 8.51)
      R=1  -> excess ~53 s,  wall ~61 s
  H_flip: binding the cap at all flips something durable, so every nonzero ratio
    costs about the same:
      R=40 -> wall ~21 s AND R=1 -> wall ~21 s

  These differ by a factor of 2 at R=40 and a factor of 3 at R=1, against a
  within-cell spread of ~1.5x. n=3 per ratio is enough. If R=40 lands near 21 s,
  H_prefix is dead and the fix has to address durable state, not the window
  schedule. If it lands near 10 s, the cost is bounded by 223 x 8 MiB / R and
  THAT is the documentable warning for docs/knobs.md.

  Cost of the discriminator: 6 cells x 3.78 GB = 22.7 GB = ~$0.45.

RESULT 4 — THE LADDER REFUTES BOTH PRE-REGISTERED HYPOTHESES
  Ran it. Crossovers land exactly where the formula puts them, which validates the
  lever: R=1 first window=223 at off 1870.7 MB (predicted 1870), 49.42% of reads
  capped; R=40 at off 50.3 MB (predicted 47), 1.11% of reads capped.

  ratio  capped prefix   % reads capped   walls (s)               median   excess
  0           0 MB            0.00        7.19 ... 12.49 (n=8)      8.51    ---
  40         50 MB            1.11        13.29 13.31 15.11        13.31   +4.80
  4         470 MB           12.24        17.16 ... 29.11 (n=8)    21.74   +13.23
  1        1871 MB           49.42        19.77 23.55 29.28        23.55   +15.04

  Strict monotone ordering, and two of the three gaps are cleanly separated:
  all 3 r40 cells exceed all 8 r0 cells (24/24), and all 3 r40 cells fall below
  all 8 r4 cells (24/24). r1 vs r4 overlap and are NOT distinguishable at this n.

  H_prefix predicted +1.3 s at R=40 (measured +4.80, 3.7x too small a prediction)
  and +53 s at R=1 (measured +15.04, 3.5x too large). DEAD.
  H_flip predicted +13.2 s at every nonzero ratio (measured +4.80 at R=40, 2.8x
  too large a prediction). DEAD.

  The truth is in between and strongly SATURATING: a 40x range in capped-prefix
  length (50 -> 1871 MB) produces only a 3.1x range in excess (4.80 -> 15.04 s).

  And the naive pipelining model fails in the opposite direction, which is the
  useful part. If the excess were round trips spent below a full window, then
  excess ~ (RTT/blockSize) * integral(dc / w(c)) over the ramp, and since both
  ends of the ramp scale as 1/R that integral is (RTT/R)*ln(223/floor) — excess
  proportional to 1/R, i.e. R=1:4:40 = 1 : 0.25 : 0.025. Measured: 1 : 0.88 :
  0.32. Far flatter than any window-pipelining account predicts.

  So the cost is NOT the window being small. It is triggered by the cap binding
  at all early in the read and then deepens only weakly. Upstream's own stated
  next step — audit what windowCap() binding touches on the read path BEYOND the
  window value, given SetMax is called before every Observe — is where this
  points, and the data now constrains it: whatever the durable thing is, it is
  substantially set within the first ~50 MB.

RESULT 5 — THE ONE-LINE WARNING FOR docs/knobs.md
  Across ratio 0, 1, 4 and 40, EVERY cell moved exactly 3,776,834,855 bytes in
  exactly 465 GETs with identical total dispatch (672 blocks). Same bytes, same
  requests, 2.8x spread in wall. The practical consequence:

    --readahead-evidence-ratio costs 1.6x to 2.8x wall clock on high-RTT reads of
    objects larger than the prefetch window, at EVERY ratio tested including one
    permissive enough to clear the cap after 1.1% of the object, with no change
    in bytes fetched or requests issued. You cannot tune the cost away with a
    higher ratio. In-region the same effect is +7.5% (+0.215 s, p~0.03).

  That is narrower and more actionable than what is in knobs.md now, and it is
  also the answer to upstream's framing question: the cost IS specific to objects
  larger than the window — on the 101.5-block object it showed up as an
  occasional stall, and on a 450-block object it is a deterministic factor.
