GATE 5f-M — IS THE WINDOW DIVISOR CHARGED FOR IDLE OPEN HANDLES? (lith#298)
2026-10-01, in-region, ~$0.02 of GET requests (no egress, head node already up)
================================================================================

WHY: upstream handed #298 over explicitly — "#298 is the live thread and it's yours:
the budget line reading 1250000000 bytes identically at c32, c128 and c512 means the
third bound is computed with no reference to how many slots will consume it. Three
bounds, three derivations, and now demonstrably no composition in that direction
either." They also closed #256 and parked the default block-size change in #300 on
the strength of 5f-L, so the unit question is settled and the DEPTH SIZING question
is what remains.

Of the three bounds, exactly one has never been exercised in this entire campaign:
the per-handle divisor.

  perHandleWindow() = clamp(budgetBlocks / openHandles, 2, maxReadahead)
  budgetBlocks()    = prefetchBudget / blockSize            (fs.go:1252, :1279)

Every gate from 5f-G through 5f-L ran ONE open handle, where budgetBlocks/1 = 492 >
maxReadahead = 223, so the declared depth bound won and the divisor was invisible.
It can only bind with more handles. And whether it counts handles that are merely
OPEN, versus handles that are actively streaming, decides whether `--max-readahead`
means anything at all to a real scientific reader — because netCDF/HEMCO workloads
hold a great many files open and read them one at a time.

There is a banked observation this would explain. In the original GCHP production
measurement BOTH mounts ran at the window FLOOR of 2. Rank count alone does not
explain it: 492/48 = 10, not 2. Hundreds of simultaneously-OPEN files would.

SETUP  in-region (2.2 ms), DEFAULT flags (8 MiB block, auto max_readahead -> 223),
  fresh cold mount + unique metrics port + --pf-trace per cell, --log-level info,
  n=3. 16 distinct GEOSFP A3dyn days, 3.72-3.81 GB each = 444-454 blocks of 8 MiB,
  so every object is larger than the 223-block window and can realize it in full.

  ARM A  N processes each streaming a DIFFERENT object concurrently
           N in {1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16}
  ARM B  1 streaming reader + (N-1) handles held OPEN after a single 4 KiB header
           read, never streamed.  N in {2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 64, 256}
           This is the netCDF/HEMCO shape, and N=256 is the only cell that can
           reach the clamp FLOOR of 2 (492/256 = 1.92).
  ARM C  1 streaming reader + 7 handles opened with NO read at all (N=8)
           Separates "open() registers the handle" from "the first read does".

  Observable is the window lith actually computed, from the trace's `window` column,
  not a summary: per streaming handle, wmax = max over its fetches and wsteady =
  median over the last 80% of its fetches. Arm A's readers do not start
  simultaneously, so the first reader's early fetches legitimately see fewer open
  handles; THE PREDICTION IS ABOUT wsteady and wmax is reported as an upper bound.

PRE-REGISTERED, WRITTEN AND COMMITTED BEFORE THE RUN
  H_charge: openHandles counts every OPEN handle regardless of activity. Then the
    steady-state window is clamp(492/N, 2, 223) in BOTH arms:
      N:        1    2    3    4    8   16   64  256
      window: 223  223  164  123   61   30    7    2
    Note the shape is not monotone-from-the-start: N=2 gives 246 > 223, so the
    prediction is NO CHANGE at N=2 and a 26% drop at N=3. A threshold at a named
    place is a much stronger test than "decreasing".
    Arm C also drops (open() alone registers).
  H_active: only handles with outstanding reads are counted. Then ARM A follows the
    table above but ARM B STAYS AT 223 FOR EVERY N, including 256, and arm C stays
    at 223.
  H_firstread: open() alone does not register, the first read does. Then arm B
    follows the table and ARM C STAYS AT 223 while arm B at N=8 does not.

  These differ in sign on cheap decisive cells: B at N=8 (61 vs 223) and B at N=256
  (2 vs 223) separate H_charge from H_active; C at N=8 separates H_charge from
  H_firstread.

  WHAT EACH OUTCOME WOULD MEAN
  - H_charge: `--max-readahead` is unreachable for any multi-file reader, and the
    headline knob of this entire issue is silently governed by how many files the
    application happens to have open — which is not a property anyone tunes, log,
    or documents. That makes the banked window-of-2 a bug rather than a mystery, and
    it makes the divisor, not the unit and not the evidence ratio, the thing with the
    largest reach into real workloads.
  - H_active: the divisor is doing something defensible and #298 is purely a
    reporting/derivation-disagreement issue, not a behavioural one.
  - H_firstread: both, and the fix is scoped to accounting at open().

  SECOND, INDEPENDENT PREDICTION (the one that makes it matter)
  Aggregate in-flight commitment = (streaming handles) x window x 8 MiB. Under
  H_charge: 1.87 GB at N=1, 3.74 GB at N=2, then ~4.13 GB flat for N>=3 in arm A.
  The budget line in the mount log says 1,250,000,000 bytes. So the logged budget
  would be EXCEEDED AT EVERY HANDLE COUNT — 1.5x at N=1 and 3.3x at N>=3 — which is
  the concrete demonstration #298 is missing: not that three derivations disagree on
  paper, but that the one printed as "the budget" is never the one enforced.

  REFUTER, stated in advance: if arm A's wsteady does not fall at N=3 (where 492/3 =
  164 first drops below 223), then budgetBlocks is not 492 on this box under
  multi-handle load and the whole derivation I verified at one handle does not
  generalize. I would report that and stop, rather than reach for a fourth bound.

COST  in-region, so no egress. ~475 GB of GETs is ~$0.02 of request charges on a head
  node that is already up. Upstream declined the $0.60 cross-region concurrency cells
  on the grounds that no decision hung on them; this one is both cheaper and attached
  to a live issue they just assigned to me.

RESULTS

RESULT 0 — H_charge CONFIRMED, INTEGER-EXACT, IN EVERY CELL
  Realized steady-state window vs clamp(492/N, 2, 223), median of 3 reps:

    N          1    2    3    4    8   16   64  256
    predicted 223  223  164  123   61   30    7    2
    ARM A     223  223  164  123   61   30    -    -     (N streaming readers)
    ARM B      -   223  164  123   61   30    7    2     (1 reader + N-1 held OPEN)
    ARM C      -    -    -    -    61    -    -    -     (1 reader + 7 opened, NO read)

  42/42 cells exact, every rep, both arms. Not one off-by-one. The threshold lands
  exactly where predicted: NO change at N=2 (492/2 = 246 > 223, so maxReadahead still
  binds) and a 26% drop at N=3 (164). H_active is refuted — arm B does not stay at
  223 — and H_firstread is refuted by arm C: 7 handles that were opened and NEVER
  read drive the window from 223 to 61 exactly as 7 header-reading handles do.

  So: openHandles counts every OPEN file descriptor, mount-wide, whether or not it
  has ever been read.

  Three consequences that were not in the prediction and fall straight out:
  (a) CROSS-PROCESS. The holder is a separate process from the reader. One
      process's open files shrink another process's prefetch depth, with no
      isolation and nothing in either process's view to show it.
  (b) PER-DESCRIPTOR, NOT PER-OBJECT. Arm B at N=64 held 63 fds spread over only 16
      distinct objects and realized 7 = 492/64, not 492/17 = 28. Opening the same
      file twice halves your prefetch depth.
  (c) THE LOG IS WRONG BY 111x. The mount log line "readahead window blocks=223" is
      printed identically in the N=64 and N=256 cells, where the realized window is
      7 and 2. Nothing else in the log corrects it. That is #297 with a number on it.

RESULT 1 — IT COSTS WALL TIME, AND THE COUNTERS CANNOT SEE IT
  Arm B only: ONE streaming reader, SAME object, every cell. The only thing that
  changes across the row is how many OTHER files are sitting open.

    N           2      3      4      8     16      64     256
    window    223    164    123     61     30       7       2
    wall s  3.187  3.110  3.161  3.366  3.239   7.739  20.343   (median of 3)
    MB/s     1185   1214   1195   1122   1166     488     186
    GETs      480    481  481/2  485/6    494     495     495
    fetched  3776.8 3777.9 3778.9 3783.1 3791.5 3792.6  3792.6  MB

  6.38x THE WALL for holding 255 files open, on fetched bytes that differ by +0.4%
  and a GET count that differs by +3%. Complete separation: B-n64's fastest rep
  (7.655 s) is slower than B-n16's slowest (3.674 s), and B-n256's fastest (20.090 s)
  is slower than B-n64's slowest (7.837 s).

  This is the THIRD independent instance on this issue of the same lesson: bytes and
  requests being near-identical is a true and insufficient regression gate. 5f-G/5f-I
  found it for --readahead-evidence-ratio; this is a 6.4x version of it.

  THERE IS A KNEE, AND IT IS NOT WHERE THE KNOB IS. From 223 down to 30 blocks the
  cost is not measurable (1185 / 1214 / 1195 / 1122 / 1166 MB/s — unordered inside
  noise). The cliff is between 30 and 7. So 30 blocks = 252 MiB in flight already
  saturates this path in-region, and the shipping 223 is ~7x deeper than it needs to
  be — while the regime that actually hurts is the one no one is tuning.

RESULT 2 — CAUSAL CONTROL: IT IS THE DEPTH, NOT THE DESCRIPTORS
  The obvious alternative is that holding 255 descriptors is itself expensive. So the
  same shallow windows were produced a second way, with ONE handle and no holders at
  all, by forcing --max-readahead. 4 forced depths x 8 handle counts, n=3, 32 cells:

    realized window == clamp(492/N, 2, forced_max_readahead) in 32/32 CELLS.
    The two bounds compose exactly as min(), with no interaction term.

  Pooling every cell by the window it actually realized:

    window   cells    med s    MB/s    spread
      223        3     2.92    1293    2.90-3.01   (auto, 1 handle, main gate)
       61       15     3.12    1212    2.72-4.23
       30       21     3.61    1047    3.28-5.44
        7       27     9.47     399    7.00-24.80
        2       33    25.50     148   16.06-51.34

  At ONE handle with no holders, --max-readahead 2 gives 25.67 s — SLOWER than the
  20.34 s reached at window 2 via 255 held descriptors. So holding descriptors is not
  a cost in itself; the entire penalty is the depth they cause. And at forced
  --max-readahead 2 the wall is flat across N = 1..256 (23.8-27.5 s, no trend),
  confirming handle count has no effect of its own once the window is pinned.

  WHAT I WILL NOT CLAIM: that wall is a function of realized window ALONE. The
  window-2 row spans 16.06-51.34 s, and the spread is not random — the
  mra=61/N=256 cell (16.1-18.4 s) is systematically ~1.5x faster than every other
  way of reaching window 2. Same realized steady-state window, different wall. I do
  not know what the residual is; it is not monotone in forced max_readahead
  (mra=30/N=256 is the slowest of all). Window sets the magnitude CLASS and the sign;
  something else sets ~1.5x inside it, and I am handing that over rather than fitting
  it.

RESULT 3 — THE LOGGED BUDGET IS NEVER THE ENFORCED ONE (the #298 point)
  The mount log says, verbatim and in every one of the 150 cells:
    "inflight-bytes budget" budget="1250000000 bytes (2 × 50.0 Gbps baseline × 100 ms)"
  = 1192 MiB. Realized aggregate commitment in arm A, (streaming handles) x window x
  8 MiB:

    N              1     2     3     4     8    16
    aggregate   1871  3741  4127  4127  4094  4027   MiB
    vs logged   1.57x 3.14x 3.46x 3.46x 3.43x 3.38x

  Exceeded at EVERY handle count, by 1.57x at one handle and ~3.46x once the divisor
  takes over. So the three bounds of #298 do not merely disagree on paper: the one
  printed as "the budget" is not a budget. What the divisor actually conserves is the
  RAM fraction (4.128 GB), which holds flat to within 2.4% for all N >= 3 — that part
  is working exactly as designed and arm A is the control that shows it.

RESULT 4 — THE BANKED GCHP FLOOR OF 2, EXPLAINED
  The original production measurement had BOTH mounts running at the window floor of
  2, which 48 ranks alone could not explain (492/48 = 10). The floor needs N >= 246
  open descriptors. A 48-rank GCHP run holding ~6 met/HEMCO files open per rank is
  288. Arm B at N=256 reproduces the floor exactly, and Result 1 prices it: that run
  was reading at 186 MB/s where 1293 was available, through no flag anyone set.

  This also explains, retroactively, why `--max-readahead` did nothing in the earliest
  gates of this campaign and why the whole investigation had to move to single-handle
  microbenchmarks to see any window effect at all. The knob was never reachable.

LIMITS, STATED RATHER THAN BURIED
  - IN-REGION ONLY (2.2 ms), one box, one object family, contiguous dd, 8 MiB blocks.
    At higher RTT the knee almost certainly moves deeper, so the 30-block knee is a
    number for this path, not a recommendation.
  - Arm A's aggregate throughput is NIC-BOUND (1290 -> 1769 MB/s, saturating ~14 Gbps
    on a c7g.4xlarge) and therefore says nothing about the divisor's cost. Arm A is
    here for the window observable and the aggregate-commitment arithmetic only. All
    wall claims come from arm B and the forced-window control, which stream ONE
    object and never approach the NIC.
  - n=3 throughout. Every wall claim above rests on complete separation between
    adjacent cells rather than on the magnitudes, which are soft.
  - The ~1.5x residual inside window 2 is unexplained (Result 2).

COST  ~$0.02 of GET requests. In-region, so no egress; head node already up. 475 GB
  read across 182 cells.
